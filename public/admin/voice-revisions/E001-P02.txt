E001-P02 — The Life He Never Lived

The city kept moving around him as if nothing important had happened.

Rain had begun lightly enough that Flavio barely noticed it at first. Only when the pavement around him darkened and the thin fabric of his shirt started clinging to his skin did he realize how long he had been sitting there. A sedan crossed the intersection with its wipers beating steadily across the windshield. A jeepney rolled after it, rattling hard enough that he could feel the vibration through the curb. Two motorcycles slipped between lanes, horns complaining at the traffic ahead as though the city had decided that one man collapsing beside the road was still not a good enough reason to slow down.

Flavio might have found that funny on another day.

He tried to breathe instead, and the pain in his chest immediately reminded him why he should not waste the effort.

Across the street, the buildings rose into the rain—glass, concrete, office lights still burning behind tinted windows. Somewhere up there, people were probably finishing reports, answering messages, arguing with printers, checking the time, deciding whether they had enough energy left for one more task before going home. Storefronts stayed bright below them. A convenience-store door opened and closed. Someone stepped out carrying a plastic bag, looked toward the small crowd near the curb, then disappeared behind a passing bus.

Nothing had ended.

Except, perhaps, him.

A horn sounded farther down the road, followed almost immediately by another. Someone shouted for a driver to move, and the absurdity of it sat badly in Flavio’s chest beside everything else. Not because the drivers were cruel. They simply did not know him. To them, whatever was happening ahead was traffic, a crowd, an ambulance that had not arrived yet—one more delay in a city made of delays.

Flavio understood that better than he wanted to.

On another day, he might have been one of them. He might have looked once, checked whether somebody had called for help, then checked the time because Teresa needed her medicine, Isabel was waiting, and tomorrow would still arrive whether a stranger on the street was ready for it or not.

His fingers twitched against the pavement.

His phone.

The thought struck harder than the rain.

Someone had to call home.

Teresa would wonder why he was late. At first she would blame traffic. She always gave ordinary explanations a fair chance before worrying. Then she would look at the clock again. Isabel would probably message him first, something short and irritated because worry always made her sound annoyed before it made her sound afraid.

He tried to move his arm.

His fingers scraped against the road and stopped.

“Phone,” he managed.

The word came out so weakly that he was not sure anyone heard it.

A man in office clothes crouched several steps away, already speaking into his own phone. Someone behind him repeated the street name. A woman held an umbrella awkwardly over Flavio and kept shifting it every time somebody spoke to her. Another person said not to move him until help arrived.

Good.

Someone knew what to do.

The relief bothered him more than it should have. He had spent most of his life arranging things so nobody else would ever need to know what to do after him. Medicine before it ran out. Bills before reminders. Food before the container emptied. Repairs before small problems became expensive. He had always tried to get there first.

Now strangers were doing things around him while he lay beside the road, unable to contribute anything useful.

Across the street, the enormous display changed.

A mountain city appeared beneath a sky filled with drifting lights. Bridges stretched over clouds. Players crossed a crowded market carrying weapons, cloth, food, and things Flavio could not name from this distance. Somewhere near the center, a group sat around a fire beneath a night sky so clean it looked invented.

Then the image faded into the same words.

GENESIS — LIVE MORE.

Flavio stared at them.

Cars passed beneath the screen, carrying the pale light across their roofs and windows.

Live more.

His chest tightened again.

Teresa’s medicine came to mind first.

Of course it did.

He could almost see the boxes beside the rice cooker, labels folded flat so he could compare dosage and price. He knew which pharmacy usually carried the cheaper generic, how many tablets she should have left, which expense could be moved if she needed another consultation before payday. He knew all of that with the certainty of a man who had spent years turning love into receipts.

What he could not remember was the last time he had sat beside her without glancing at a clock.

That hurt differently.

Isabel came next, not as one memory but many.

Waiting by a doorway. Sending him a message he meant to answer after work. Pretending she was not disappointed when he arrived late. Telling him it was fine before he could apologize properly. Saying, “Next time,” so often that eventually the words sounded like something they both believed.

He had always thought there would be another chance because ordinary life encouraged that belief. Tomorrow came so regularly that people treated it as property.

He had done the same.

Nico had asked him to eat after work more times than Flavio could count. Sometimes he refused because he was tired, sometimes because he had chores, sometimes because spending money outside when there was rice at home felt irresponsible. There had always been a reason.

That was the problem.

Every reason had been real.

Every sacrifice had made sense while he was making it.

Put together, they had become his life.

A vehicle stopped hard near the intersection, tires hissing across the wet road. People shifted. Someone called for space. Flavio turned toward the sound and noticed that the buildings no longer had clean edges.

He blinked.

The blur remained.

The giant screen was still bright enough to survive it. Everything else had begun to soften.

The crowd opened suddenly, and someone moved quickly through it.

A young woman dropped beside him.

At first Flavio saw only her outline against the headlights. Her hair was wet along one side, and light from the Genesis display caught against it before disappearing when she leaned closer. He tried to focus on her face, but his vision refused to hold it together. Eyes. A mouth. The pale movement of one hand hovering above him, uncertain where to touch.

She looked frightened.

That confused him.

He did not know her.

At least he was almost certain he did not.

“Can you hear me?”

Her voice reached him more clearly than her face.

Flavio tried to answer. A breath escaped, but no words followed.

She leaned closer.

“Stay with me.”

Stay.

For some reason, his first impulse was to apologize.

He had somewhere to be.

Home.

Teresa would be waiting. Isabel would probably be irritated. There was likely food covered on the table beneath one of those cheap plastic food covers Teresa refused to throw away because it still worked perfectly well.

Something ordinary.

Something he had eaten a hundred times without noticing that one day he might want that exact meal more than anything in the world.

The woman looked over her shoulder and called to someone. Flavio could not make out the answer. The sounds of the street had begun separating from one another—engines farther away, footsteps nearer, the electronic chirp of the pedestrian signal, rain striking plastic, voices rising and falling around him.

Then her voice again.

“Stay with me.”

He looked at her because she had asked him to.

The effort felt absurdly difficult.

Her face wavered in the city light. A strand of wet hair clung to her cheek. Something bright caught near one eye and disappeared before he could decide whether it was rain.

Flavio wanted to know what she looked like.

The thought came unexpectedly.

He had passed thousands of faces in his life without caring whether he remembered any of them. He had walked through crowds thinking about bills, schedules, repairs, medicine, work—always something waiting beyond the person directly in front of him.

Now the last face beside him refused to stay clear.

“What…”

His voice barely existed.

The woman bent closer.

“What?”

Flavio’s eyes slipped past her shoulder.

The screen changed again.

A green valley opened beneath a gold sky. A road stretched toward distant towers, and figures ran along it with the reckless confidence of people who believed the road would still be there when they reached the horizon.

GENESIS — LIVE MORE.

What was life?

The question returned.

Earlier, he had tried to answer it with large words.

Love. Happiness. Misery. Pain.

Now, with the rain cooling his face and the city fading around him, the answer seemed embarrassingly small.

Warm food at a table. Teresa complaining that he worked too much. Isabel asking twice because she still hoped he might say yes. Nico keeping a chair empty for him. A morning with nowhere urgent to go. Ten quiet minutes in front of an electric fan after fixing it, listening to the motor run cleanly.

Things he had never thought needed protecting.

The woman took his hand.

He did not notice exactly when.

Her fingers were warm.

Flavio tried to squeeze back.

His hand barely moved.

“The ambulance is close,” someone said.

The woman nodded quickly, then looked at him again.

“Help is coming.”

Her voice broke slightly on the last word.

Flavio stared at her blurred face.

Why did that matter to her?

Who was she?

He wanted to ask, but his lungs had become miserly. There was not enough air left for curiosity.

His gaze drifted back toward the giant screen.

LIVE MORE.

He had spent years waiting for enough—enough money, enough savings, enough stability, enough time, enough room in his life to finally say no when somebody asked for one more shift. Enough of everything before he allowed himself anything.

There had never been enough.

The word almost offended him now.

Enough had always lived somewhere ahead of him.

Life had been where he already was.

If I had another chance…

The thought did not finish.

Darkness pulsed at the edge of his vision.

The woman tightened her grip.

“Stay with me.”

Flavio tried.

He really tried.

Not because he believed help would arrive in time. Not because he suddenly understood what was happening. He tried because someone had asked him for one thing, and for once there was nothing else competing for his attention.

Stay.

He looked at her face, then the screen, then back at her again. Neither stayed clear.

If I could live more…

Not wealth. Not power. Not another job.

God, definitely not another job.

Just time.

Time to sit when Teresa told him to eat. Time to answer Isabel before the second message. Time to ask Nico how he was doing and actually wait for the answer. Time to remember Rogelio without turning every memory into another lesson about work. Time to waste one afternoon and not punish himself for it afterward.

The buildings disappeared first. Their windows stretched into pale lines, and the traffic lost its shape until there was only sound. The people around him became shadows moving through rain.

The Genesis screen remained.

So did the woman’s voice.

“Stay with me. Stay with me.”

Flavio could no longer tell whether his eyes were open.

“Help is coming.”

He wanted to tell her he heard her. He wanted to tell her not to look so afraid. He wanted somebody to tell Teresa. Somebody to tell Isabel. Somebody—

The thought slipped away.

All he could still make out was the light behind the woman.

LIVE MORE.

If I have a second chance…

No.

Even now, he was trying to make a plan.

Typical.

The thought almost made him smile.

If I can live more…

Her hand tightened around his.

“Stay with me.”

Flavio let out the breath he had been fighting to keep.

The rain vanished with it.

The traffic, the buildings, the lights, the giant screen, and the woman whose face he had never managed to see all disappeared together.

This time, Flavio did not borrow another breath from tomorrow.

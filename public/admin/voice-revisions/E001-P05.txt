E001-P05 — One Step

By the time Flavio left the kitchen, the house no longer felt impossible.

Not normal, exactly. Nothing about waking seventeen again could become normal in a single morning, and he was not foolish enough to pretend otherwise. But the strangeness had softened around the edges. Teresa was alive. Isabel was loud. The old house still creaked in the same places. Rogelio’s pliers still sat on the shelf. Breakfast had been real enough to burn his tongue.

For the first time since opening his eyes, Flavio felt something close to steady.

He went back to his room to change for school.

The uniform hanging from the side of the wardrobe looked smaller than he remembered, though that was probably because thirty-five-year-old Flavio had forgotten how thin seventeen-year-old Flavio had been. He changed slowly, more distracted by the familiar stiffness of the fabric than he wanted to admit. The collar scratched slightly against his neck. One button took two tries because his fingers moved faster than the old shirt allowed.

Everything around him carried some tiny insult from the past.

Even his school bag felt too light.

Flavio checked the contents before putting it on. Notebooks. Pens. A folded worksheet. Coins in the small pocket. Nothing unusual.

Then something appeared in front of him.

He stopped.

There was no sound, no flash, no dramatic light filling the room. The words simply existed.

[OBJECTIVE]

Walk 1,500 steps.

Progress: 0 / 1,500

Flavio stared at the message.

For several seconds, he forgot to breathe.

The text hung in his vision with the clean, unnatural sharpness of something that did not belong in the room. He looked past it toward the wardrobe. The words remained.

He turned his head.

They moved with him.

Not floating in the air, then.

Flavio raised one hand and waved it through the text.

Nothing happened.

Of course nothing happened.

That would have been too convenient.

He stepped backward.

The message stayed where it was in his sight.

He closed one eye.

Still there.

Then the other.

Still there.

Flavio stood in the middle of the room with one shoe half tied, trying very hard not to react like a man who had just died, returned to the past, and immediately developed floating instructions.

He failed.

“What the hell?”

No answer.

He waited.

Nothing.

The house continued around him. A chair scraped from the kitchen. Isabel called something to Teresa. Outside, a motorcycle passed the lane.

The message remained.

Flavio looked at it again.

Walk 1,500 steps.

That was all.

No explanation, no reward, no timer, no warning, no glowing button inviting him to accept.

Just an objective.

He frowned.

At thirty-five, he had become very familiar with instructions that appeared without explanation.

Supervisors did that.

Bills did that.

Life did that.

Apparently miracles had management styles too.

Flavio finished tying his shoe and stood straight.

The progress remained at zero.

He took one step.

Progress: 1 / 1,500

Flavio stopped.

His eyes narrowed.

He moved one foot backward.

Progress: 2 / 1,500

He stepped forward again.

3 / 1,500

Flavio stared at the number.

So it counted deliberate movement.

Or maybe any step.

He shifted sideways.

4 / 1,500

Another.

5 / 1,500

He looked down at his feet, then back at the objective.

There was something deeply suspicious about how simple it was.

Flavio took five more steps around the room.

10 / 1,500

He stopped near the desk.

The number did not change while he stood still.

Good.

At least it was not counting breathing as walking.

He turned toward the door, then hesitated.

Could anyone else see it?

The thought came late enough to irritate him.

Flavio walked into the hallway with the objective still hanging in his vision.

Isabel was near the table, putting something into her bag.

She looked up.

“What?”

Flavio glanced at the message, then at her.

“You don’t see anything?”

Isabel looked around.

“Like what?”

“Nothing.”

She stared at him for a moment.

“You’re still weird.”

“I know.”

Flavio walked past her.

The counter increased.

13 / 1,500. 14 / 1,500. 15 / 1,500.

He slowed.

So movement through the house counted too.

That made sense.

Probably.

Which was not the same as proving anything.

He went to the sink, picked up his water bottle, filled it, and returned to the hallway.

Progress climbed naturally.

24 / 1,500.

Flavio watched the number with growing suspicion.

He did not like instructions he did not understand.

Not anymore.

At seventeen, he might have followed something like this because it looked important. Thirty-five-year-old Flavio had spent too many years learning how expensive blind obedience could become.

He returned to his room.

The objective followed him.

No new text appeared.

Flavio sat at the desk and pulled out one of the notebooks.

He turned to a blank page.

At the top, he wrote:

PRIVATE LOG

Then beneath it:

Unknown objective appeared after waking.

Walk 1,500 steps.

Progress counts ordinary walking.

Current count: 31.

Flavio paused.

He had almost written system.

He crossed the word out before finishing it.

Too early.

He did not know what this thing was. He did not know whether it came from Genesis. Genesis had not launched yet. He did not know whether it had anything to do with his return.

He did not even know whether he was sane.

Flavio looked at the message again.

Still there.

He wrote another line.

Do not assume.

That felt better.

He leaned back and considered the problem.

One thousand five hundred steps was not much. A walk to school could probably cover a large part of it. He could finish it quickly if he wanted.

That was exactly why he did not want to do that yet.

Easy instructions were still instructions.

Flavio had spent enough of one life letting urgency decide things for him.

He tapped the pen against the notebook.

What happened if he ignored it?

Nothing so far.

No countdown.

No warning.

What happened if he completed it?

Unknown.

What happened if he deliberately walked in circles until it finished?

Also unknown.

He looked toward the doorway.

School would force him to walk anyway.

That seemed safer.

Not because the objective was safe.

Because school existed whether the objective existed or not.

He would let ordinary life test the strange thing first.

That decision settled something in him.

Flavio wrote:

Do not change routine only to obey unknown objective.

Observe first.

He stopped.

That sounded like something an old man would write.

Isabel had been right.

He tore the page out.

Then stared at it.

No.

That was stupid.

He put it back.

The page stayed.

Good.

He would need records.

If impossible things were going to happen around him, he was not trusting memory alone.

Especially not his memory.

Eighteen years was a long time.

He remembered the shape of his life.

Not every detail.

That distinction mattered.

Flavio closed the notebook and slid it into his bag.

The objective remained.

31 / 1,500.

He stood.

32.

He picked up the bag.

33.

Flavio paused.

“Of course.”

No response.

He adjusted the strap and headed for the door.

Teresa was near the sink when he reached the kitchen.

“You have everything?”

Flavio checked his bag automatically.

“Probably.”

Teresa looked at him.

“Probably?”

He sighed.

“Yes, Ma.”

“Lunch?”

He opened the small front pocket.

There.

“Yes.”

“Money?”

“Yes.”

“Notebook?”

Flavio looked at the bag.

Several notebooks.

Including one now containing a private log about impossible floating text.

“Yes.”

Teresa nodded, satisfied.

Then she pointed toward his hair.

“Fix that.”

Flavio touched it.

It had apparently recovered from his earlier attempt.

Isabel passed behind him already wearing her bag.

“Leave it. It matches his personality.”

Flavio turned.

“What does that mean?”

“It looks confused.”

She walked past him before he could answer.

Flavio stared after her.

Teresa tried not to laugh.

“You’re encouraging her again.”

“I’m not saying anything.”

“You’re smiling.”

“That is not illegal.”

Flavio shook his head.

For a few seconds, he forgot about the objective completely.

That felt good.

Then he stepped outside.

Progress: 41 / 1,500.

The lane was brighter now.

Morning traffic had begun to thicken, though traffic here meant tricycles, motorcycles, pedestrians, neighbors standing too close to the road, and children moving in groups toward school. Flavio knew the route.

His feet knew it even better.

He walked.

The count rose quietly.

52. 61. 73.

Flavio stopped looking after that.

There was something unpleasant about watching every step become a number.

He had done enough of that with money.

Enough pesos for rice. Enough for medicine. Enough for transportation. Enough days before payday.

He did not need to turn walking into another debt.

So he left the objective at the edge of his attention and followed Isabel toward the main road.

She walked ahead of him for a while, then slowed.

“You’re quiet.”

Flavio glanced at her.

“You complained I was weird when I talked.”

“You’re still weird.”

“That seems unfair.”

“It’s accurate.”

Flavio smiled.

They walked a little farther.

The objective continued counting.

109 / 1,500.

Isabel kicked a small stone out of the path.

“Are you really okay?”

The question came casually, but not carelessly.

Flavio looked at her.

She was still young enough to pretend concern was curiosity.

“I’m okay.”

She studied him.

“You looked like you were going to cry earlier.”

“I had something in my eye.”

“In the kitchen?”

“Yes.”

“What?”

“Emotion.”

Isabel groaned.

“That was terrible.”

“I thought it was good.”

“It wasn’t.”

Flavio laughed.

That answer felt better than the truth.

What could he tell her?

I died. You grew up. I missed things. Now I’m back and something invisible is counting my footsteps.

Maybe later.

Much later.

For now, he simply walked beside her.

The neighborhood opened into a busier street. Vendors were setting up. A man pushed a cart loaded with bottled drinks. Students crossed in groups, uniforms bright under the morning sun. Somewhere nearby, oil hissed in a pan.

Flavio remembered this route.

Not perfectly.

Enough.

He had walked it hundreds of times.

At seventeen, it had felt long.

At thirty-five, it felt painfully short.

That bothered him.

So much of youth had felt endless while he was living it.

School days. Walks home. Waiting for dinner. Weekends.

At thirty-five, entire years had collapsed into a handful of memories.

Now one street held more detail than some whole years of his adult life.

He slowed slightly near a small repair stall.

Tools hung from hooks along the wall.

A man inside was tightening something with a wrench.

For one second, Flavio thought of Rogelio.

Not the day he died.

Just hands.

Callused fingers.

Metal clicking against metal.

A voice telling him to hold something properly.

Flavio kept walking.

He did not need to turn every memory into grief.

Some things were allowed to remain memories.

Isabel had already moved several steps ahead.

“Kuya!”

“I’m coming.”

He quickened his pace.

The counter climbed.

243 / 1,500.

Flavio noticed it, then deliberately looked away.

Not yet.

He would not let the thing dictate his morning.

At the corner, Isabel turned toward another path.

“I’m going this way.”

Flavio nodded.

“Okay.”

She took two steps, then looked back.

“You’re sure you’re okay?”

Flavio almost gave the automatic answer.

I’m fine.

The lie he had used until the end.

He stopped himself.

“I don’t know yet.”

Isabel blinked.

That was apparently not the answer she expected.

Flavio smiled.

“But I’m not dying.”

The joke came out before he could stop it.

He immediately regretted it.

Isabel frowned.

“That’s a weird thing to say.”

“Yes.”

“Very weird.”

“I know.”

She shook her head and walked away.

Flavio watched until she disappeared into the stream of students.

His smile faded slowly.

Not dying.

He had done that already.

He turned toward school.

The objective still waited.

278 / 1,500.

He continued walking.

The sun was warm against his face. His bag rested against one shoulder. Students moved around him. Some talked too loudly. Some looked half asleep. One boy was trying to finish homework while walking, which seemed like an excellent way to fail both tasks at once.

Flavio almost warned him.

Then remembered he was seventeen.

Nobody wanted life advice from a seventeen-year-old who looked equally unprepared.

By the time the school building came into view, the counter had reached 416.

Flavio slowed at the gate.

He looked at the number.

Still incomplete.

Good.

Nothing had forced him. Nothing had punished him. Nothing had changed because he refused to walk in circles until the task ended.

That was useful.

Not proof.

Useful.

He entered the campus with the rest of the students.

The count continued.

421. 422. 423.

Flavio left it alone.

Classes would begin.

He would move.

Eventually, 1,500 would arrive.

What happened after that could wait until it actually happened.

For now, the objective had asked him to walk.

Flavio intended to do something much more difficult.

He was going to live the day.

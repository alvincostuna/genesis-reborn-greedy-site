E002-P01 — One Thousand Five Hundred

The number followed Flavio all the way to school.

It never blocked the road or hid anyone’s face, and if he stopped looking for it, the pale display seemed to settle at the edge of his awareness. That almost made it worse. A loud thing could be ignored eventually; a quiet thing that waited for attention felt patient in a way he did not trust.

He had left the house with the objective at forty-one steps. By the end of the first block, it had climbed again.

Flavio stopped beneath the awning of a closed shop, glanced at the road to make sure he was not standing in anyone’s way, then checked.

Progress: 86 / 1,500

He had not spent the last several minutes deliberately obeying anything. He had simply walked toward school.

So ordinary movement counted.

Flavio looked down at his shoes.

That was almost disappointing.

Part of him had expected the impossible thing to demand impossible behavior. Instead, it had apparently decided that walking was sufficient.

A tricycle passed close enough to make him step back.

Progress: 87 / 1,500

Flavio frowned.

“Of course.”

The driver did not hear him.

He continued toward school.

The strange thing about being seventeen again was how often his memory lied about scale. Streets he remembered as long were short. Buildings he remembered as large seemed smaller. Even the distance to school, which had once felt like a meaningful portion of the morning, now seemed like something he could cross before finishing one adult complaint about traffic.

His body disagreed.

After several blocks, his calves were already working harder than he expected. The younger body was not injured or sick. It was simply underused, and the sensation embarrassed him because he remembered treating seventeen as proof that he had been healthy by default.

He had been young.

That was not the same thing.

At thirty-five, he had used the opposite mistake. He had treated exhaustion as proof that he was working hard enough.

Flavio shifted the bag on his shoulder and kept walking.

He did not check the counter again until he reached school.

The front gate was crowded with students arriving in small groups, some talking loudly enough to force everyone around them into the conversation. Flavio moved with the flow, listening more than speaking. A classmate called his name, and he answered automatically. Another gave him a look because Flavio had responded before the second call.

Apparently younger Flavio had not been famous for alertness in the morning.

Inside the classroom, the objective remained.

Flavio sat down.

The count stopped.

Good.

He opened his notebook and wrote only the current number in one corner, small enough that anyone glancing over would assume it belonged to something ordinary.

Then class began.

He expected the display to distract him.

It did not.

That surprised him.

He listened to the teacher, copied what needed copying, answered one question too quickly, and received another suspicious look from a classmate who remembered yesterday’s version of him better than Flavio did. The objective remained where it had been, quiet and incomplete.

No warning appeared.

No timer.

No penalty.

By the time the break bell rang, Flavio had almost forgotten it was there.

Almost.

He went to the washroom, crossed the covered walkway, and stopped near an empty wall.

Progress: 638 / 1,500

Flavio stared.

Ordinary walking around school had counted everything.

He had not been thinking about the objective while moving between rooms, standing in line, or returning to his seat. The thing had simply recorded what he did.

That bothered him more than if it had demanded constant attention.

He pulled out the private notebook, turned to the page he had started that morning, and wrote:

Ordinary movement counts.

No visible punishment for ignoring objective.

Progress continues during normal routine.

He hesitated, then added:

Do not stare at display while crossing traffic.

That one was less scientific.

It was also useful.

A voice came from behind him.

“Why are you hiding?”

Flavio shut the notebook.

Isabel stood several steps away, holding her own notebook against her chest.

“I’m not hiding.”

“You are standing beside a wall during break.”

“That is called standing.”

She looked at the notebook he had just closed.

“What are you writing?”

“Notes.”

“For what?”

Flavio considered several answers and chose the least troublesome.

“Something I’m testing.”

Isabel stared at him.

“That is not reassuring.”

“Good.”

“Why good?”

“You should not be reassured by vague statements.”

She made a face.

“You really do sound old now.”

Flavio almost smiled.

Isabel lifted her notebook instead.

“I showed the solution.”

“What solution?”

“The math problem.”

It took him half a second.

Breakfast.

Her notebook.

The line he had pointed to.

“Right.”

She opened to the page and held it toward him.

“I showed the working. Teacher said it was correct.”

Flavio looked at the steps.

“You fixed the skipped part.”

“I told you I knew what I was doing.”

“You told me after asking for help.”

“I changed my mind.”

“You seem to do that often.”

“So do you.”

Flavio looked up.

Isabel grinned before he could ask what she meant.

“You looked less strange this morning.”

“That is not what you said this morning.”

“I was being kind.”

He laughed despite himself.

She closed the notebook.

“Are you coming home after class?”

“Yes.”

“Straight home?”

Flavio paused.

He had been thinking about the objective again.

Isabel noticed immediately.

“There! That face.”

“What face?”

“The one where you stop hearing people.”

“I heard you.”

“What did I say?”

“You asked whether I was going straight home.”

She narrowed her eyes.

“Fine. You pass.”

Flavio shook his head.

“Generous.”

The rest of the school day continued without anything dramatic happening, which Flavio appreciated more than he would have before dying. Lessons ended. Chairs scraped. Students packed bags. Someone argued about a missing ruler. Another person tried to borrow money and promised to return it with the confidence of someone who had no immediate plan to do so.

Ordinary life was noisy.

Flavio liked it.

He and Isabel walked home together for most of the route. She complained about the ruler, then about a classmate who had apparently misunderstood the meaning of borrowing.

“Did you ask for it back?”

“Not yet.”

“Why not?”

“I’ll ask tomorrow.”

“You should ask today.”

“It’s already tomorrow for school.”

“That sentence makes no sense.”

“It makes enough sense.”

Flavio looked at her.

She bumped his shoulder.

“Don’t make the worried face.”

“I’m not worried.”

“You are always worried.”

That landed more cleanly than she intended.

Flavio looked ahead.

In his first life, Isabel had learned to stop mentioning it.

This Isabel had not.

Good.

“I’ll try to use a different face.”

“You only have that one.”

“That seems genetically unlikely.”

She laughed and moved ahead when she saw someone she knew near the corner.

“I’ll see you at home!”

Flavio raised a hand.

“Okay.”

He watched her leave.

The objective waited.

Progress: 1,186 / 1,500

Only a few hundred remained.

He could finish them quickly.

That thought came with the old pressure immediately: finish it now, remove it from the list, do not leave work hanging.

Flavio recognized the feeling.

He did not like recognizing it.

So he went home first.

Teresa had left a covered plate on the table.

“You came straight back,” she said from the laundry area.

“I said I would.”

“You also said you would check the latch.”

“I will.”

“After eating.”

Flavio looked at the plate.

He was hungry.

That should not have felt like permission.

He sat and ate.

The food disappeared faster than breakfast had. His younger body had spent a day walking, sitting, standing, climbing stairs, and apparently keeping an invisible counter pleased.

Teresa noticed the empty plate.

“You’re hungry again.”

“Yes.”

She looked suspiciously satisfied.

Flavio decided not to comment.

After lunch, he checked the door latch.

The temptation was to repair it immediately because he had promised. Instead he tested it properly. Teresa told him it caught more after rain. He checked the hinge, the worn contact point, and the way the door sat in the frame.

The old pliers would help.

Forcing the adjustment before he had enough light would not.

“I’ll do the full repair tomorrow,” he said.

Teresa raised an eyebrow.

“Tomorrow?”

“Yes.”

“You are voluntarily leaving work unfinished?”

Flavio looked at her.

“It still locks.”

She tested it herself.

It did.

“Fine,” she said. “Tomorrow.”

The world did not end.

That was becoming a theme.

By late afternoon, Flavio had only a small number of steps left.

He told Teresa he was going for a short walk.

“Short,” she repeated.

“Yes.”

“Not a race.”

“Yes.”

“You already walked all day.”

“I know.”

Teresa gave him the look mothers used when they were allowing something while reserving the right to say they had warned you.

Flavio left.

The lane had softened under late-afternoon light. Children were outside. Someone was washing a motorcycle. A neighbor swept dust toward a pile with the determined expression of someone engaged in a personal dispute with dirt.

Flavio walked at an easy pace.

The count rose.

1,322.

1,361.

1,417.

His calves were tired, but not in a dangerous way.

At 1,482, he slowed.

He could have finished the remaining steps in less than a minute. Instead he stood under the shade of a wall and waited for his breathing to settle.

Nothing happened.

No warning appeared.

No punishment.

The display simply waited with him.

Flavio drank water.

That simple patience unsettled him.

His first life had trained him to expect rest to cost something. If he stopped working, work remained. If he slept, time disappeared. If he said no to a job, someone else took the money.

This objective did not care.

Walk 1,500 steps.

That was all.

Flavio resumed when he was ready.

1,493.

1,494.

He shortened his stride experimentally.

The count did not change.

He took a proper step.

1,495.

Another.

1,496.

He almost laughed.

“Fine.”

A neighbor glanced at him.

Flavio pretended he had not spoken to an invisible counter.

1,497.

1,498.

1,499.

He stopped.

The final number waited.

So did the street.

Nothing pushed him forward.

He could step now.

Tomorrow.

Never, perhaps.

The choice was still his.

Flavio looked toward the old house.

Then he stepped.

Progress: 1,500 / 1,500

[OBJECTIVE COMPLETE]

Walk 1,500 steps.

[CONDITION RECORDED]

Flavio waited.

No light.

No sound.

No strength poured into his body.

No reward window appeared.

The neighbor continued sweeping.

A dog barked farther down the lane.

Flavio read the last line again.

Condition recorded.

“Recorded where?”

Nothing answered.

He took several more steps.

No new counter.

He walked ten more.

Still nothing.

At that point his legs suggested the experiment had become stupid.

Flavio agreed.

He went home.

At the table, he drank water and opened the private notebook.

Ordinary travel counted.

Delay produced no visible penalty.

Completion produced no visible reward.

Condition recorded — destination unknown.

He stared at the last line.

The old version of him would have filled the rest of the page with theories until one sounded convincing.

He left the space blank.

Teresa set another small plate near his elbow.

“You walked enough to be hungry again?”

Flavio looked at the food.

Then at her.

“I was hungry before I went.”

“Then you should have said so.”

He closed the notebook.

The objective had finished.

The questions had not.

That was fine.

For once, Flavio did not need an answer before dinner.

E002-P04 — Learning How to Learn

Flavio’s notebook became useful the day he stopped trying to solve the mystery.

Before that, too many pages had been wasted on questions that sounded important and produced nothing.

Why him?

Why seventeen?

Why did the private interface appear before Genesis launched?

Who had sent it?

Could it read his thoughts?

Was it connected to his death?

Was he inside a dream that had learned how to charge school fees?

None of those questions had answers.

So Flavio made a rule.

Write what happened.

Separate what he saw from what he thought it meant.

That rule saved paper.

It also saved him from himself.

The private interface had developed a small vocabulary over the year.

OBJECTIVE.

CONDITION.

COMPLETE.

FAILED CONDITION.

INVENTORY.

DURABILITY.

The words arrived without instructions.

Flavio hated that.

He also respected it.

An unexplained thing could still be tested if it behaved consistently.

That was how the durability experiment began.

He was holding an old practice tool when a pale line appeared beside it.

[DURABILITY]

Flavio stared.

The value meant something.

That was all he knew.

He turned the object over, checked for cracks, tested the grip, and wrote down the visible condition. Then he used it normally.

The number did not change.

He used it again.

Still nothing.

When the worn section finally degraded in a way that mattered, the durability value changed.

Flavio sat back.

“That’s annoying.”

No one answered.

He added another page to the notebook.

Possible relationship to physical condition.

Do not assume remaining uses.

Do not assume hidden structural damage.

Do not assume repair restores full value.

He stopped.

That last line had not been tested.

He crossed it out.

Better.

The biggest test came several weeks later.

Flavio set his phone on a shelf, aimed the camera toward the wall, and started recording.

The private interface hovered clearly in his vision.

On the phone screen, there was nothing.

Flavio moved.

The interface followed.

The recording showed a seventeen-year-old boy walking through a room and staring with intense concentration at empty air.

Flavio stopped the video.

He watched three seconds.

Then deleted it.

Some experiments produced evidence.

Others produced material Isabel could use against him for the rest of his life.

He tried a photograph.

Nothing.

A screenshot.

Nothing.

Screen recording.

Nothing.

Then, because he did not fully trust his own phone, he borrowed Nico’s.

The excuse was weak.

“My camera is behaving strangely.”

Nico handed him the phone.

“What did you do to it?”

“Nothing.”

“That answer suggests you did something.”

“I need the camera.”

“Why?”

“To test the camera.”

Nico stared at him.

“You have become impossible to talk to.”

“Temporarily.”

“That does not comfort me.”

Flavio repeated the test.

Nothing.

No device recorded the private overlay.

He returned Nico’s phone.

“Did it work?”

“No.”

“What were you testing?”

Flavio thought about the floating words only he could see.

“Something private.”

Nico’s face changed immediately.

“Oh.”

Flavio saw the conclusion arrive.

“Not that.”

“I did not say anything.”

“You were about to.”

“I was about to respect your privacy.”

“You were smiling.”

“That is still allowed.”

Flavio left before the conversation became worse.

Back home, he opened the notebook.

Capture test: negative.

Visible to me, absent from recorded image.

Unknown whether perception-only or inaccessible layer.

Do not label hallucination or projection without evidence.

He underlined the last line.

That was when Nico arrived carrying printed pages and enough excitement for three people.

“You are not ready.”

Flavio closed the notebook.

“For what?”

“Genesis announced persistent regional NPC schedules.”

“That sounds useful.”

Nico stared.

“Useful?”

“Yes.”

“That is your response?”

“What response would satisfy you?”

“I don’t know! Wonder? Awe? Recognition that we are about to enter the first persistent virtual world where characters remember what players do?”

Flavio held out a hand.

“Give me the pages.”

Nico looked offended by the lack of ceremony, but handed them over.

Flavio read.

Persistent local schedules.

Adaptive quest responses.

Player reputation.

Craft condition.

Dynamic market behavior.

Item durability.

His eyes stopped.

Nico noticed.

“What?”

“Durability.”

“Yes?”

“Confirmed.”

Nico stared.

“What are you talking about?”

“Genesis uses item durability.”

“Of course it does. They showed equipment wear months ago.”

Flavio kept reading.

The private interface had used the same word before he had paid attention to public Genesis documentation.

That did not prove a connection.

Inventory and durability were common system words.

But Flavio wrote the relationship down later.

Possible terminology overlap.

Possible.

Not confirmed.

Nico dropped into the chair beside him.

“They also published access pricing.”

Flavio looked up.

“How much?”

Nico’s enthusiasm weakened.

“Before you react—”

“How much?”

“The cheapest Tier One package is thirty thousand.”

Flavio stared.

Nico raised both hands.

“There is financing.”

“How much down?”

“Six thousand.”

“Monthly?”

“Twelve payments of two thousand.”

Flavio calculated once.

Then again because anger sometimes made arithmetic less trustworthy.

It did not.

₱6,000 down.

₱24,000 over twelve months.

₱30,000 total.

“Thirty thousand is not cheap.”

“I did not say cheap.”

“You said cheapest.”

“That is different.”

“It should not be.”

Nico sighed.

“It is Genesis.”

“That does not reduce the number.”

“No, but it explains the number.”

“Marketing explains many crimes.”

“That is not legally a crime.”

“Then marketing has excellent lawyers.”

Nico laughed despite himself.

Flavio kept reading.

In his first life, this would have ended the conversation.

Thirty thousand pesos for a game device would have been ridiculous.

Now the private interface existed.

Genesis launched soon.

The words overlapped.

And Flavio had already died once after ignoring nearly everything that did not fit neatly into survival.

He was not willing to ignore this.

That did not mean buying it.

Not yet.

“What exactly comes with Tier One?”

Nico leaned forward immediately.

Now he was in familiar territory.

He explained access hardware, account registration, baseline sensory settings, warranty limits, and launch-region availability. Flavio asked questions nobody in promotional videos seemed excited to answer.

Replacement cost.

Power draw.

Maintenance.

Financing penalties.

Transfer restrictions.

What happened if the unit failed.

Nico stared at him halfway through.

“You are evaluating a fantasy world like a refrigerator.”

“Refrigerators are expensive.”

“This is more exciting than a refrigerator.”

“That remains unproven.”

Nico groaned.

But he answered.

Over the next weeks, Flavio separated his notes into two sections.

PRIVATE LOG.

PUBLIC GENESIS NOTES.

He never mixed them.

On the public side, he wrote only confirmed information from official releases or demonstrations.

NPC schedules.

Economy systems.

Item durability.

Crafting.

Travel.

Regional servers.

Player limits.

On the private side, he wrote only what he personally observed.

Objectives.

Condition records.

Invisible overlay.

Non-recordability.

Terminology overlap.

Unknown origin.

There were rumors everywhere.

Forums claimed hidden classes had already leaked.

Someone insisted the launch build contained a secret dragon race.

Another post said players could permanently lose real memories if they died three times in one day.

Nico sent that one to him with three laughing messages.

Flavio read it.

Then deleted it from his notes.

No source.

No evidence.

No value.

That became another rule.

Excitement did not upgrade a rumor into information.

One evening, Nico found Flavio comparing the two notebooks.

“You know,” Nico said, “normal people prepare for games by watching trailers.”

“I watched one.”

“You complained about the pricing.”

“That was in the trailer.”

“It was not.”

“It should have been.”

Nico leaned over the table.

“What are you actually looking for?”

Flavio closed the private notebook before Nico could see it.

“A pattern.”

“Between what?”

“Things I know and things I don’t.”

Nico blinked.

“That is an extremely wide category.”

“I know.”

He looked at the two notebooks.

For the first time since waking at seventeen, the mystery felt manageable.

Not because he understood it.

Because he had stopped demanding that he understand everything at once.

He could learn one fact.

Test one behavior.

Reject one assumption.

Then continue.

Rogelio had taught him something similar with tools.

Do not force what you have not understood.

Flavio had ignored that lesson in more places than he wanted to admit.

Now he wrote it at the top of a new page.

Observe first.

Name later.

Genesis launch was getting closer.

The private interface remained silent that night.

Flavio did not mind.

He had enough to study without inventing answers.

E002-P05 — The Night Before Genesis

The night before Genesis launched, the private interface disappeared.

Flavio noticed because he had stopped checking for it.

That was the irony.

For almost a year, the pale messages had appeared often enough to become part of the background of his life. Not constant. Not demanding. Just present when they had something to say.

Then, on the final evening before Genesis went live, there was nothing.

No objective.

No condition.

No quiet line of text waiting at the edge of his vision.

Flavio stood in front of the mirror after washing his face and looked at himself.

Seventeen still.

Healthier than the boy who had woken in that room a year earlier.

Not transformed.

Just cared for.

He turned his head slightly as though the interface might appear from a different angle.

Nothing.

“Fine,” he said.

The mirror did not answer.

From the kitchen, Isabel shouted, “Are you talking to yourself again?”

Flavio opened the door.

“No.”

“That sounded exactly like talking to yourself.”

“I was speaking to the room.”

“That is worse.”

He ignored her and went to dinner.

Genesis had taken over the city outside.

Advertisements played on giant screens. Stores had launch displays. Nico had sent enough messages that Flavio had muted the conversation for an hour simply to finish homework.

Even Teresa knew the launch time.

Not because she cared about the game.

Because Nico had explained it during dinner two nights earlier with the seriousness of a man announcing national policy.

Teresa placed food on the table.

“Nico is coming?”

“He said he would call.”

“Not come here?”

“He has to set up his unit.”

Isabel looked at Flavio.

“You sound disappointed.”

“I do not.”

“You do.”

Flavio sat down.

He was not disappointed.

Probably.

The access unit in his room had cost more than he liked remembering.

He had saved.

Worked.

Compared financing.

Refused several bad offers.

And, eventually, bought one.

Not because Genesis looked fun.

That still irritated Nico.

Flavio had bought it because the private interface existed before launch and kept using words that Genesis later used publicly.

That was enough to investigate.

Not enough to believe.

He had spent the last year learning the difference.

Teresa put rice on his plate.

“You’re sure this thing is safe?”

Flavio looked at her.

That was the question he had asked himself most often.

“The official safety certification passed.”

“That is not what I asked.”

He paused.

“No.”

Teresa’s expression tightened.

Flavio corrected himself.

“I mean, I can’t be completely sure.”

“That is better.”

Isabel pointed at him with her spoon.

“He’s been practicing honesty.”

“I noticed,” Teresa said.

Flavio frowned.

“I have always been honest.”

Isabel laughed.

Teresa laughed too.

Flavio looked between them.

“Why is that funny?”

“Eat,” Teresa said.

He ate.

The meal was ordinary enough to make the launch feel distant.

Teresa complained about a neighbor who had returned a container without its lid.

Isabel argued that this was not a real problem.

Teresa explained why it was absolutely a real problem.

Flavio listened.

A year ago, he would have checked the time every few minutes.

Tonight he did not.

Genesis could wait through dinner.

That alone felt like proof that something had changed.

After eating, Flavio washed his plate.

Teresa watched him.

“You’re nervous.”

“I’m not.”

“You wash plates faster when you are nervous.”

Flavio looked down.

He had not known that.

Isabel leaned into the kitchen doorway.

“You also check your phone without unlocking it.”

Flavio looked at the phone in his hand.

He had just done exactly that.

“This house is invasive.”

“You live here,” Teresa said.

“That does not make surveillance acceptable.”

Isabel grinned.

“You sound like Nico now.”

“That is insulting.”

His phone rang.

Nico.

Isabel pointed.

“Speak of the disease.”

Flavio answered.

Nico’s face filled the screen.

“You ready?”

“No.”

There was a pause.

“What do you mean, no?”

“I mean I’m not ready.”

“You had a year!”

“I had a life.”

Nico stared at him.

“That was disgustingly responsible.”

“I know.”

“Are you sick?”

“Apparently.”

Nico moved the phone, showing cables and the access unit behind him.

“Mine is ready. Connection test passed. Account active. Regional selection confirmed. Hiraya start zone.”

Flavio nodded.

His own account had the same regional start.

They had planned that much.

Nico leaned closer.

“Tell me you are excited.”

“I am curious.”

“That is not the same.”

“It is what I have.”

“Fine. Tell me you are violently curious.”

Flavio smiled.

“Go finish your setup.”

“Yes, sir.”

Nico ended the call.

The house quieted later.

Isabel went to her room.

Teresa reminded Flavio twice not to stay awake until morning.

The second reminder came from the hallway.

“I heard you the first time.”

“I know.”

“Then why repeat it?”

“Because I know you.”

Flavio looked toward the door.

“That is unfair.”

“It is experience.”

Her footsteps moved away.

Flavio sat beside the Genesis unit.

The device looked less impressive without advertising around it.

No glowing portal.

No impossible magic.

Just engineered hardware, cables, padding, sensors, safety indicators, and a price that still offended him.

He ran one final check.

Power stable.

Connection stable.

Account confirmed.

Emergency disconnect accessible.

Water nearby.

Phone charged.

Door unlocked.

He stopped.

The old Flavio would have made another checklist.

This Flavio had already made three.

Enough.

He leaned back.

The room was quiet.

Then the private interface appeared.

Flavio sat upright.

For the first time all day, words hovered in front of him.

[CONDITION]

Enter Genesis.

Flavio stared.

His heartbeat changed immediately.

No explanation.

Of course.

No reward.

Of course.

No mention of why the interface had spent a year conditioning his body, teaching him to test assumptions, and using vocabulary that increasingly matched the game he was about to enter.

Just two words.

Enter Genesis.

Flavio looked at the access unit.

Then at the message.

A year of restraint tried to survive inside him.

It managed.

Barely.

He opened the notebook.

Private interface returned immediately before launch.

Condition: Enter Genesis.

He underlined condition.

Then he wrote:

No proof that private interface = Genesis.

He stared at the line.

It looked increasingly lonely.

Flavio closed the notebook.

Launch approached.

His phone filled with messages from Nico.

TEN MINUTES.

SEVEN.

FIVE.

Flavio ignored the next one.

Outside, the neighborhood continued.

A dog barked.

Someone closed a gate.

A motorcycle passed.

The city did not know that Flavio Reyes was about to step into the world advertised above the street where he had died.

Maybe that was good.

He put the headset on.

The padding settled around him.

A calm system voice confirmed safety checks.

Official Genesis text appeared.

Not the private interface.

Different style.

Different placement.

Different feeling entirely.

That mattered.

Flavio watched each official prompt carefully.

Identity confirmation.

Sensory calibration.

Pain threshold notice.

Emergency disconnect.

Regional synchronization.

Nico sent one final message.

SEE YOU INSIDE.

Flavio smiled.

Then Genesis reached launch.

The official display changed.

[GENESIS SERVICE ONLINE]

[CONNECTING TO HIRAYA REGION]

Flavio waited.

His room faded slowly.

The private message remained visible a moment longer than everything else.

[CONDITION]

Enter Genesis.

Then the world disappeared.

For one breath, there was nothing.

The last indicator blinked once, steady and green. Flavio let his shoulders loosen, listened to the distant house settle around him, and allowed himself one final breath before the transition completed quietly.

Then wind touched his face.

Before the connection completed, Flavio heard Teresa moving in the hallway outside his room. The sound was faint beneath the system audio, but he recognized it immediately: cupboard, glass, the soft tap of something set on the table.

Home remained there.

That mattered enough that Flavio almost disconnected before Genesis finished loading, just to make sure he had not forgotten something.

He checked the real-world status indicator instead.

Safe.

Connected.

Alarm active.

The room had not disappeared simply because his senses were somewhere else.

A year earlier, Flavio would have considered that interruption inconvenient. Now the awareness steadied him. Genesis could become enormous without needing to replace the life outside it.

Nico’s message flashed once more.

DON’T TAKE AN HOUR READING THE TUTORIAL.

Flavio smiled.

He typed one final reply before the interface locked for transition.

No promises.

Three dots appeared immediately.

Then vanished as the connection took priority.

The official loading space darkened around him.

For a second, Flavio remembered the giant screen above the road where he had died. LIVE MORE had looked cruel then, almost insulting in its timing. Now the same idea felt different. He was not entering Genesis because a slogan had taught him how to live. A year with Teresa, Isabel, Nico, school, repairs, sleep, food, and a body he had stopped treating like equipment had already done that.

Genesis was simply the next place he had chosen to enter.

Not the place that would save him.

Not the place he owed his second life to.

Just a world waiting on the other side of the connection.

Then wind touched his face.

E003-P01 — Genesis Is Live

The words CONNECTING TO GENESIS vanished.

For one breath, Flavio saw nothing.

His hand tightened around the edge of the chair in his real room, or at least he thought it did. The sensation disappeared almost immediately, replaced by the unmistakable feeling of standing upright. Air moved against his face. Something solid supported his weight. When he shifted one foot, the ground pushed back.

Flavio kept his eyes closed.

He had expected a loading screen, a warning, maybe one final agreement nobody read carefully enough. Instead, the room had simply fallen away.

He took one slow breath.

Then opened his eyes.

Daylight spread across a broad stone plaza.

Flavio squinted.

The sky looked too clear to be trusted.

That was his first thought.

His second was that this was a stupid first thought.

People surrounded him, all of them new arrivals by the look of it. Some stared at their hands. One man bent his knees twice, then laughed loudly when the ground did not vanish under him. A woman several steps away turned in a slow circle, eyes wide, while someone beside her kept repeating, “This is insane,” like the sentence might eventually become enough.

Flavio understood the problem.

The place felt present.

Not realistic.

Present.

Warm air touched his skin. The stone beneath his boots had small irregularities he could feel when he shifted weight. Voices came from different directions instead of flattening into sound. Somewhere beyond the crowd, a bell rang once, then again.

He looked down at himself.

Plain tunic.

Plain trousers.

Boots.

A small satchel.

A short sword at his side.

The sword caught his attention first, which annoyed him.

He had entered a world no one had ever stood inside before, and his mind immediately asked whether the starter weapon could be repaired cheaply.

Typical.

Flavio touched the satchel strap instead.

It moved under his fingers.

The official Genesis interface appeared when he focused on it.

[GENESIS]

Player: Flavio Reyes

Level: 1

Class: None

Profession: None

HP: 100%

SP: 100%

Inventory available.

Flavio read every line twice.

No hidden bonus.

No strange title.

No secret class.

No message thanking him for dying correctly.

Good.

He opened the inventory.

Starter Tunic.

Starter Trousers.

Starter Boots.

Starter Satchel.

Beginner Sword.

Basic starting supplies.

That was all.

He closed it.

The official interface had borders, icons, depth, a polished structure that made it feel designed by people who expected millions of users to complain if one button was misplaced.

The private interface from the previous year had never looked like this.

That mattered.

Flavio searched the edge of his vision anyway.

No pale objective.

No condition.

No quiet instruction.

Nothing.

He did not know whether to feel relieved.

That irritated him more than fear would have.

A player materialized beside him and immediately stepped into Flavio’s space.

Both of them moved aside at the same time.

“Sorry,” the stranger said.

“It’s fine.”

They nearly moved into each other again.

The stranger laughed awkwardly and went left.

Flavio went right.

That tiny collision made the world feel more convincing than the sky.

Other people occupied space here.

His position mattered.

He stepped away from the arrival point before someone else loaded into the same crowd.

The plaza had a name.

Hiraya Arrival Plaza.

The words appeared naturally when he focused on the location.

Flavio let the name settle without pretending it told him anything useful beyond where he stood.

He looked around.

Stone paths left the plaza in several directions. Buildings rose beyond the outer edge, layered with signs, awnings, balconies, smoke, and movement. New players spread outward like water finding cracks.

A few ran immediately.

Flavio watched one of them trip.

Not badly.

Enough.

The player stood up quickly and looked around to see who had noticed.

Flavio looked away.

He had spent too much of the last year learning not to confuse speed with progress.

That lesson apparently applied here too.

His first impulse was still to hurry.

Opening hour.

First day.

Millions of players entering at once.

Somewhere, surely, there were opportunities that could disappear if someone else reached them first.

Flavio could feel that old hunger forming before he had any idea what he was hungry for.

Scarcity without an object.

That was dangerous.

He stayed where he was long enough to breathe.

Teresa had told him before login not to skip lunch because of a game.

Isabel had told him to bring back something interesting.

Nico had told him they would contact each other after entering.

Three different instructions.

Only one of them had sounded like a command.

Teresa’s.

Flavio smiled.

He had set an alarm in the real world.

He would obey it.

That decision made the plaza easier to look at.

He tested his balance.

Bent his knees slightly.

Turned.

The satchel shifted against his hip.

Everything felt natural enough that his real-world conditioning mattered, but not so familiar that he could assume the rules were identical.

He reached toward the sword.

Then stopped.

There were too many people nearby.

Drawing a weapon in a crowd just to see how it felt seemed like the kind of mistake someone would later describe as “obvious.”

Flavio left it where it was.

A player nearby was doing exactly the opposite.

The man drew his sword, swung once, nearly clipped another newcomer, then apologized repeatedly while everyone around him created a larger circle.

Flavio watched.

Then quietly added one rule to memory.

Open space first.

He did not need a notebook for that.

Not yet.

The private interface had trained him to record facts instead of performing intelligence for himself.

That habit remained.

He knew what he had.

He knew where he stood.

He knew the official Genesis system was visually different from the private one.

He had not accepted a quest.

He had not earned experience.

He had not received any reward.

He had not discovered anything special.

That was the truth of his first minute inside Genesis.

It was enough.

He looked toward the paths leaving the plaza.

Nico was nowhere obvious in the crowd.

Flavio could have searched.

Instead he remembered the actual agreement.

Talk after entering.

Not meet at a place neither of them had known in advance.

Nico would contact him.

Or Flavio would contact Nico.

There was no emergency.

He opened the communication interface, confirmed it existed, then closed it again.

The world around him kept moving.

An NPC porter pushed a cart through the edge of the plaza and complained when two players blocked the route. The players moved quickly.

Flavio watched the cart pass.

NPC.

Probably.

The man looked annoyed enough to be real.

Flavio almost laughed.

He had read public announcements about persistent schedules and adaptive responses. Standing in front of a working person who wanted the road cleared felt different from reading those words on paper.

A group nearby began arguing over which exit was best.

One player said the north route had better early rewards.

Another asked where he had read that.

The first player said, “Everyone knows.”

Flavio walked away.

That answer had ruined enough lives outside games.

He moved toward the open side of the plaza, slowly enough to notice where he had come from and quickly enough not to turn caution into paralysis.

The stone underfoot changed slightly near the edge.

The crowd thinned.

For the first time, he could hear the wider city beneath the noise of new arrivals.

Metal.

Footsteps.

Vendors.

A distant hammer.

Someone laughing.

A door closing.

He stopped once and looked back.

The place where he had spawned was already occupied by new players.

His beginning had lasted less than a minute before the world used the space for someone else.

That felt right.

Genesis had not paused for him.

Flavio adjusted the satchel strap.

He looked forward again.

No private message appeared.

No destiny announcement.

No secret path opened.

Good.

He had entered Genesis with a starter set, a level of one, no class, no profession, and more questions than answers.

For once, that did not feel like being behind.

It felt like beginning.

He took another few steps and stopped near the edge of the arrival flow. From there, he could finally see enough of the city to understand why the plaza had felt smaller from the center. Streets opened beyond the buildings at angles he could not judge yet. Signs hung above doors in scripts he could read through Genesis translation, though the presence of translation itself raised questions he decided not to chase on his first minute.

A vendor shouted at someone for blocking a stall.

Two players argued about whether an NPC had remembered one of them from a beta demonstration.

A child ran after a rolling hoop between adults and was caught by the shoulder before reaching the road.

None of it waited for Flavio to understand.

That helped.

He had spent too much of his first life believing he needed a complete plan before he could make a safe move, then compensating by rushing once the plan failed to appear. Genesis gave him another option simply by existing around him. He could move while remaining uncertain.

He checked the sword again without drawing it. The weight against his hip was a reminder that this world expected action eventually.

Eventually was not now.

For now, Flavio chose one path that kept the arrival point visible behind him and began walking.

Not because he knew it was the best path.

Because standing forever would teach him less than taking a careful step.

No private instruction appeared to congratulate the choice.

Flavio preferred it that way.

He had entered Genesis with a starter set, a level of one, no class, no profession, and more questions than answers.

For once, that did not feel like being behind.

It felt like beginning.

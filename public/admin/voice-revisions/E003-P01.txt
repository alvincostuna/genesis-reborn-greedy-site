E003-P01 — Genesis Is Live

Wind touched Flavio’s face.

Not simulated air from a fan.

Not the flat cooling flow from the access unit.

Wind.

It carried the smell of stone, smoke, cooking oil, leather, wet wood, and too many people packed into one place.

Flavio opened his eyes.

Hiraya Arrival Plaza spread around him.

For several seconds, he forgot every question he had prepared.

Buildings rose in uneven rows beyond the plaza, tiled roofs and hanging signs layered beneath a wide morning sky. Players appeared in brief flashes of light and immediately began moving in every direction. Some shouted names. Some laughed. Someone nearby turned in a slow circle with both hands raised, apparently overwhelmed by the existence of fingers.

A bell rang somewhere deeper in the city.

Flavio stood still.

The ground beneath his boots had texture.

Not just visual texture.

He could feel the slight unevenness of stone through the soles.

He moved one foot.

Weight shifted naturally.

His body inside Genesis was not identical to his real one, but it responded closely enough that a year of conditioning suddenly felt less excessive.

Nico appeared several meters away.

He materialized facing the wrong direction.

“Flavio!”

Flavio looked at his back.

“Nico.”

Nico spun around.

“Oh.”

“You were shouting at a wall.”

“I was checking acoustics.”

“You were lost for four seconds.”

“That is not enough time to be lost.”

“It was enough.”

Nico ignored him and looked around.

His face changed.

The joking disappeared.

For a moment, he simply stared.

Flavio understood.

Genesis was beautiful.

Annoyingly beautiful.

The promotional videos had made it look polished. The real thing felt occupied.

NPCs moved through the square without waiting for players to finish being impressed. A porter pushed a cart around a group blocking the path and complained loudly. Vendors opened shutters. A woman carrying bread stepped aside when two new players nearly collided with her, muttered something under her breath, and kept walking.

The city did not feel like a stage waiting for them.

It felt busy without them.

Nico whispered, “This is insane.”

Flavio nodded.

“Yes.”

Nico looked at him.

“That was almost emotion.”

“Don’t ruin it.”

The official interface appeared when Flavio focused.

[GENESIS]

Player: Flavio Reyes

Level: 1

Class: None

Profession: None

HP: 100%

SP: 100%

Inventory available.

Flavio read every line.

Then he looked away.

The interface folded naturally to the edge of his vision.

Different.

That was his first useful observation.

Genesis menus had a visual weight the private interface did not. They carried icons, borders, depth, and clear system hierarchy. The private messages had always looked almost too simple.

He opened the inventory.

Starter Tunic.

Starter Trousers.

Starter Boots.

Starter Satchel.

Beginner Sword.

A few basic items.

Nothing unexpected.

Nico opened his own interface.

“Oh, this is clean.”

Flavio looked at him.

“You have been inside for less than a minute.”

“I can appreciate design quickly.”

“Apparently.”

Nico equipped his weapon with unnecessary enthusiasm.

Flavio inspected his.

The beginner sword felt light.

Too light, almost.

Not because it lacked weight, but because the balance was cleaner than the improvised tools Flavio had spent years handling in real life. He adjusted his grip.

The blade followed smoothly.

He loosened his hand.

Better.

Rogelio’s old lesson returned.

Don’t fight the tool.

Flavio smiled faintly.

Nico noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You smiled at the sword.”

“I adjusted my grip.”

“That is somehow worse.”

Flavio ignored him.

The private interface had not appeared.

He checked without meaning to.

Nothing.

Genesis was live.

Official menus surrounded him.

The mysterious system that had followed him for a year was silent.

That should have separated the two cleanly.

It did not.

Flavio walked toward the edge of the plaza.

The city responded around him.

A child NPC ran between two stalls and was scolded by a merchant.

A guard redirected arriving players away from a loading zone.

A player tried to jump onto a cart and was shouted down by the owner.

Nico laughed.

“He got yelled at.”

“He climbed onto someone’s property.”

“It’s an NPC.”

The cart owner turned.

“What did you call me?”

Nico froze.

Flavio looked at him.

The owner pointed at the road.

“Move.”

Nico moved.

Flavio waited until they were several steps away.

“Persistent NPC responses.”

“Do not.”

“You wanted wonder.”

“I do not want this version of wonder.”

Flavio laughed.

They reached a quieter side of the plaza where several public information boards stood.

Official maps.

Starter notices.

Safety rules.

Quest postings.

Market directions.

Flavio studied them.

Nico groaned.

“We just arrived.”

“Yes.”

“Can we walk around first?”

“We are walking around.”

“You are reading municipal notices.”

“They are part of the city.”

“No normal person enters the greatest virtual world ever made and reads posted regulations first.”

Flavio looked at him.

“Then normal people will learn them later.”

Nico pointed toward a group already sprinting out of the plaza.

“Those people are going adventuring.”

“One of them has no weapon equipped.”

The group reached the street.

A guard stopped the unarmed player and sent him back.

Nico looked away.

Flavio smiled.

The public map showed Hiraya’s central districts, starter routes, gate locations, and several marked service areas.

No hidden locations.

No secret route glowing for him.

Good.

He had worried about that.

Not because secret knowledge would be useless.

Because he did not have any.

Flavio’s memories of Genesis came from a life in which he mostly watched advertisements, heard other people talk about it, and ignored opportunities to play.

He knew broad history.

Not detailed mechanics.

He was not walking into a solved world.

That made the city more interesting.

Nico leaned over the map.

“East gate?”

“Maybe.”

“Why maybe?”

“I want to see the request board first.”

Nico sighed.

“Of course you do.”

They moved through the plaza.

Flavio checked prices as they passed stalls.

Not because he planned to buy anything.

They had almost nothing.

That made prices more important.

Water.

Basic food.

Repair items.

Cheap consumables.

Starter gear.

The numbers were small.

The differences were not.

A stall near the plaza charged more for the same ration sold farther down the road.

Convenience.

Traffic.

Urgency.

Flavio remembered.

Nico caught him looking.

“You’re comparing prices.”

“Yes.”

“We have no money.”

“Exactly.”

Nico rubbed his face.

They reached the request board.

Players crowded around it.

Some chose the highest reward immediately.

Others argued over which quest had the best experience.

Flavio waited until he could actually read.

The official interface responded.

Public requests opened.

Starter deliveries.

Local errands.

Low-risk collection work.

Pest clearing.

A marsh request with a reward large enough to attract half the nearby players.

Flavio read the route description.

Longer travel.

Unfamiliar ground.

Higher consumable risk.

More competition.

He closed it.

Nico pointed.

“That one pays more.”

“Yes.”

“So why close it?”

“Because it pays more.”

Nico stared.

“That sentence is illegal.”

“It attracts more people.”

“So?”

“More people means more competition. Longer travel means more time. Unknown terrain means more ways to make mistakes.”

Nico looked at the reward again.

“It still pays more.”

“The number is not the whole cost.”

Nico sighed.

“I missed the version of you who made bad decisions casually.”

“You never met that version.”

“That is unfortunately true.”

Flavio opened another request.

[PUBLIC REQUEST]

Outer Verge Pest Clearing

Type: Starter Field Request

Objective:
Defeat 3 registered Common pests in the eastern verge.

Recommended Range:
New players / low-risk field zone

Completion:
Return after objective confirmation.

Reward:
60 EXP
8 Copper

Flavio read it twice.

Three targets.

Nearby field.

Starter zone.

Fixed reward.

Enough exposure to test combat without pretending he was ready for anything impressive.

Nico leaned closer.

“That one?”

“Probably.”

“Probably?”

“Close. Clear objective. Known beginner zone.”

“And only eight copper.”

“We currently have zero.”

“That is a depressing argument.”

“It is accurate.”

Flavio accepted.

[QUEST ACCEPTED]

Outer Verge Pest Clearing

Objective:
Defeat 3 registered Common pests.

Progress:
0 / 3

Reward:
60 EXP
8 Copper

The official window folded away.

Flavio waited.

Half a second.

One second.

Nothing private appeared.

Nico noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That is the second time today you said nothing like it has history.”

Flavio started toward the eastern route.

“It doesn’t.”

“That answer definitely has history.”

They passed the first line of vendors.

Flavio slowed near field rations.

Nico noticed again.

“You’re memorizing prices.”

“Yes.”

“You cannot buy anything.”

“That makes prices more relevant, not less.”

“You are exhausting.”

“So I’ve been told.”

At the outer gate, Flavio stopped.

He adjusted the satchel so it would not swing across his hip.

Checked the sword.

Looked at the ground.

Packed earth replaced stone beyond the gate. Grass grew along a drainage line. Rocks broke the surface near the road.

Promotional material had promised realistic movement.

Realistic movement meant realistic ways to trip.

Flavio stepped outside Hiraya.

The field opened wide.

Wind moved through waist-high grass.

The road curved around a low rise.

Beginners chased small creatures with varying levels of competence.

One swung too early and missed.

Another ran downhill after a target, lost footing, and rolled.

Nico inhaled deeply.

“Adventure.”

Flavio watched the fallen player stand and check whether anyone had seen him.

“Gravity.”

“That is your response to a fantasy world?”

“It appears operational.”

Nico laughed.

Flavio smiled.

Something moved in the grass ahead.

Both of them stopped.

Low body.

Fast lateral motion.

A pause.

Then another shift.

Nico reached for his weapon.

Flavio drew his sword more slowly.

“Finally,” Nico whispered.

Flavio kept his eyes on the grass.

“Watch before running.”

This time, Nico listened.

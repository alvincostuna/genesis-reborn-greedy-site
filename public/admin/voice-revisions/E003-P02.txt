E003-P02 — The World Loads Around Him

Hiraya Arrival Plaza grew larger once Flavio had to cross it.

From the center, it had looked simple enough: stone, buildings, paths, people. Walking through it turned that impression into something less tidy. Players stopped without warning. Others followed strangers who looked confident. New arrivals appeared in waves, some careful, some loud, some already running as though the first hour would close permanently if they did not move fast enough.

Flavio kept to the edge of the crowd.

That gave him room to stop without becoming someone else’s problem.

He wanted a picture of the place, not a perfect map.

There was a difference.

A perfect map would take time he did not need to spend.

A useful picture meant knowing where he had arrived, which direction the city opened, where he could return, and what kinds of information were actually available.

The stone beneath his feet had a faint unevenness. The satchel brushed his hip as he moved. The sword stayed secured.

Flavio liked that last part.

The more convincing Genesis became, the less interested he was in testing a blade around strangers.

A knot of players near one path argued loudly.

“East first!”

“Why east?”

“Because everyone’s going east!”

“That’s not a reason!”

“It means something is there!”

Flavio kept walking.

Nico would have enjoyed that conversation.

Flavio did not.

Following a crowd could be useful.

Following it because it was a crowd was different.

He stopped near a low stone marker where he could still see the arrival point behind him.

Good.

He could return without opening a map.

That was enough orientation for now.

A player nearby took three careful steps, bent both knees, then jumped.

When nothing dramatic happened, the player laughed.

Flavio smiled.

A year ago, he had tested a private objective in his bedroom with the same mixture of fear and embarrassment.

Here, everyone was awkward together.

That made Genesis feel almost kind.

The official interface opened when he asked for the local map.

It showed the immediate plaza and recognized public routes.

No secret roads.

No private markers.

No hidden icons that belonged only to him.

Good.

He had worried, somewhere in the back of his mind, that reincarnation would come with knowledge he did not earn.

It had not.

Flavio remembered Genesis as a cultural event from his first life, not as a solved game. He knew the name. He knew it became enormous. He knew people made money, lost money, formed guilds, built reputations, and spent years inside it.

That knowledge was broad enough to be dangerous if he pretended it was specific.

He closed the map.

A vendor shouted from farther down the street.

Another voice answered.

An NPC guard redirected a cluster of players away from a loading lane.

The crowd reacted naturally enough that Flavio almost forgot he was supposed to distinguish player from NPC.

He watched one man step into the road while staring at a menu.

A cart driver stopped sharply.

“Move!”

The player jumped aside.

“Sorry!”

The driver muttered and continued.

Flavio looked after him.

Persistent schedules.

Adaptive responses.

Nico had practically shouted those phrases into Flavio’s face months earlier.

Seeing them move through a crowded street made the words smaller and more believable.

Genesis did not need to announce that it was alive.

People were already getting in its way.

Flavio moved farther from the arrival point.

He passed an official information board.

Starter directions.

Local services.

Basic safety notes.

Public request access.

Market district arrows.

Nothing mysterious.

Flavio read them anyway.

A player beside him glanced over.

“First time?”

Flavio looked at him.

“Yes.”

The player laughed.

“Obviously. Me too.”

That was fair.

The stranger pointed toward one route.

“Someone said the best quests are that way.”

“Who?”

The player paused.

“I don’t know.”

Flavio nodded.

“Good luck.”

He walked the other direction.

Not because the information was wrong.

Because it was not information yet.

He reached a quieter section of the plaza.

The buildings felt closer here.

Signs hung from wooden brackets.

Smoke drifted from somewhere that smelled like grilled meat.

Flavio’s stomach reacted immediately.

He laughed under his breath.

A virtual smell had just reminded his real body that lunch existed later.

Teresa would appreciate the betrayal.

He checked the alarm indicator.

Still plenty of time.

Good.

He continued.

The starting equipment sat differently as he moved longer distances.

The satchel strap pulled slightly across one shoulder.

The boots felt firm but not uncomfortable.

The sword’s presence altered how he turned.

Small things.

Useful things.

Flavio filed them away without turning each one into a theory.

He had spent a year learning not to do that.

The private interface had not returned.

He checked once.

Nothing.

That absence remained strange.

But Genesis itself was giving him enough to pay attention to.

He did not need to force the mystery into every corner.

A boyish voice called his name.

“Flavio!”

He turned.

Nobody nearby was looking at him.

The voice came again through the official communication system.

Nico.

Flavio opened it.

“You alive?”

Nico’s face appeared in the call window.

Flavio looked around.

“Yes.”

“Where are you?”

“Hiraya Arrival Plaza.”

Nico stared.

“Yes, I know that. Which part?”

Flavio looked at a nearby marker.

“East side. Near an information board.”

Nico turned his own view.

“I see three information boards.”

“Then your problem is now better defined.”

“Do not start.”

Flavio smiled.

Nico continued.

“I’m near a statue.”

Flavio looked around.

“There are two.”

Silence.

Then Nico laughed.

“This is going well.”

“Stay where you are.”

“Why me?”

“Because you are more likely to move while explaining where you are.”

“That is offensive.”

“Is it wrong?”

Another pause.

“No.”

Flavio opened the map.

This time, he used it with a purpose.

Statue.

Two possible locations.

Nico described the nearby buildings.

Flavio matched them.

“Wait there.”

“I said that first.”

“You did not.”

“I was about to.”

Flavio closed the call.

He walked.

The map helped.

So did having already oriented himself.

Several minutes later, he found Nico leaning against the base of a stone monument with the expression of a man who had been waiting several hours instead of several minutes.

“You took forever.”

“You were twelve minutes from me.”

“I could have walked.”

“And become twenty minutes from me.”

Nico pushed away from the monument.

“Maybe.”

Flavio looked at him.

Nico’s Genesis avatar resembled him enough to be recognizable, though a little cleaner than real life had any right to make him.

Nico noticed the inspection.

“What?”

“You look expensive.”

“That is the nicest thing you have ever said.”

“It was not a compliment.”

“Still counts.”

They started walking together.

Nico immediately began talking about everything he had seen.

The sensory calibration.

The interface.

The crowd.

The NPCs.

The weapons.

A player who had tried to climb a market stall and been chased away by the owner.

Flavio listened.

That was part of why Nico was useful.

He experienced the world outwardly.

Flavio tended to fold everything inward until it became a problem to solve.

Together, they noticed more.

They passed a small vendor row.

Nico slowed.

“Food.”

“We have starter supplies.”

“I can still look.”

“You can look.”

“That sounded like permission.”

“It was not.”

Nico laughed.

Flavio checked prices.

One stall charged slightly more for a basic ration than another farther from the busiest path.

He noticed.

Did not write it down.

Did not stop.

Just remembered.

Convenience cost money.

Even in Genesis.

Some laws survived worlds.

They reached another official board.

This one included public requests.

Nico brightened immediately.

“Now we are doing something.”

Flavio read the headings.

“Reading is doing something.”

“No. Reading is what you do before doing something.”

“That sounds dangerous.”

“Everything sounds dangerous when you say it.”

Flavio smiled.

The first quest list opened.

Simple errands.

Deliveries.

Local collection.

Pest control.

A few routes farther from the plaza.

Nico leaned closer.

“There.”

He pointed toward one with a higher reward.

Flavio read it.

Longer route.

Less familiar area.

More travel.

Unknown competition.

He closed it.

Nico stared.

“That pays more.”

“Yes.”

“So?”

“So we do not know what the extra pay is buying.”

Nico rubbed his face.

“Sometimes I miss being alone.”

“You called me.”

“That was before I remembered this.”

Flavio opened a simpler task.

Pest control.

Nearby.

Clear count.

Starter range.

No mystery.

He did not accept it yet.

He read it.

Then read it again.

Nico waited.

Flavio looked toward the route leading away from the plaza.

The world had finished loading around him.

Nico studied the quest board with the solemn expression of a man selecting a national economic policy.

“What if we just choose the one with the coolest name?”

“No.”

“What if the coolest name also pays more?”

“Still no.”

“What if—”

“No.”

“You are destroying the spirit of adventure.”

Flavio looked toward the road leading out of Hiraya.

“I think the spirit of adventure will survive basic reading.”

Nico sighed theatrically, but stayed.

That mattered more than the complaint. Nico could have run after the first group and probably would have in another mood. Instead he stood beside Flavio and read the same public information, occasionally pointing out things Flavio had overlooked.

One request required a delivery through streets neither of them knew.

Another offered more copper but carried a longer route and a recommended level they did not have.

A third looked easy until Nico noticed the collection area overlapped with a heavily crowded path.

Flavio glanced at him.

“You’re learning.”

“Do not sound proud. I resent it.”

Flavio smiled.

Working together changed the board. Alone, Flavio tended to reduce choices into costs and uncertainty. Nico noticed people, crowding, and practical annoyances Flavio might otherwise label later. Neither perspective was complete.

They did not call themselves a party.

Not yet.

They were simply two friends trying not to embarrass themselves in the most expensive game either of them had ever entered.

Flavio read the pest-control request one more time.

Clear objective.

Nearby route.

Known count.

Unknown combat.

That was enough uncertainty for one decision.

He did not accept it yet.

He looked toward the exit first, memorized the direction back to the plaza, then returned his attention to the board.

The world had finished loading around him.

Now he needed to decide what he was willing to do inside it.

E003-P02 — The World Loads Around Him

The field beyond Hiraya looked calm from the gate.

That impression lasted until Flavio watched a player miss a rabbit-sized creature, stumble forward, and get bitten on the ankle hard enough to scream.

Nico winced.

“So much for calm.”

Flavio looked across the grass again.

The starter zone was not dangerous in the way people used that word for cliffs, storms, or armed men. It was dangerous in the simpler way: enough moving pieces existed that carelessness could become pain quickly.

Players spread across the verge in uneven groups. Some ran after anything that moved. Others stood together comparing interfaces. A few had already learned to wait.

Flavio preferred those.

He and Nico stayed near the road at first.

The eastern verge rolled outward in low grass, scattered stones, shallow ditches, and patches of brush thick enough to hide something knee-high. Beyond that, the land rose gently toward a line of trees.

No glowing arrows marked the perfect route.

No secret landmark appeared for Flavio.

The official map showed only what it had promised.

Road.

Gate.

Starter field.

Quest boundary.

That was reassuring.

Nico pointed toward a cluster of players.

“Should we follow them?”

“Why?”

“They look like they know where they’re going.”

Flavio watched the group split in three directions.

“They do not.”

“Fair.”

They moved farther from the gate.

The city noise weakened behind them. Wind moved through the grass. Flavio heard insects, distant voices, metal striking something, and once, a player shouting triumphantly before another voice yelled that the monster was not dead yet.

Nico laughed.

“That one learned quickly.”

Flavio adjusted his sword.

“Hopefully.”

The official quest remained at the edge of his interface.

Outer Verge Pest Clearing

Progress:
0 / 3

Reward:
60 EXP
8 Copper

He looked at it once, then dismissed it.

Three kills sounded simple.

Simple objectives had already taught him not to confuse simple wording with simple execution.

A movement flickered near the ditch.

Flavio stopped.

Nico stopped a step later.

“What?”

Flavio raised one hand.

The grass moved again.

Low.

Quick.

Then still.

A creature emerged halfway from cover and disappeared again.

Nico whispered, “Gnawer?”

“Probably.”

“You say probably too much.”

“It prevents embarrassment.”

“Does it?”

“No.”

Nico grinned.

Flavio moved closer to the roadside where the ground was firmer.

They watched.

The creature surfaced again several meters away.

Genesis identified it after Flavio focused long enough.

[TARGET IDENTIFIED]

Verge Gnawer

Level: 2

Rank: Common

The panel folded aside.

Nico breathed out.

“Level two.”

“We are level one.”

“That sounded like concern.”

“It was information.”

“That is your favorite disguise.”

Flavio ignored him.

The Gnawer was ugly in a practical way. Coarse fur. Long body. Front teeth large enough to justify the name. It moved low to the ground, stopping often before changing direction.

Flavio watched the legs.

Nico watched Flavio.

“Are we attacking?”

“Not yet.”

“Why?”

“I want to see how it moves.”

“It moves like a rat that hates us.”

“That is useful only emotionally.”

“I contribute morale.”

The Gnawer darted sideways.

Flavio tracked it.

Pause.

Shift.

Another pause.

It did not attack.

He frowned.

The pattern was not obvious.

Good.

Obvious patterns were easy to invent when people wanted one.

Another player approached from the far side.

The Gnawer turned toward him.

The player rushed.

The creature moved left, paused, then launched from the right.

The player blocked badly and still stayed upright.

Flavio watched the hind legs compress.

There.

Maybe.

He did not trust one example.

The other player chased the Gnawer away.

Nico looked at Flavio.

“You saw something.”

“I saw it jump.”

“That is not what your face says.”

“What does my face say?”

“That you are filing paperwork internally.”

Flavio sighed.

They kept moving.

The field itself became a lesson.

Low grass hid holes.

Packed earth offered better footing.

Players clustered near visible spawns, which meant any monster there attracted multiple swords immediately.

The quieter edges had fewer people but more blind spots.

Flavio chose neither extreme.

They walked along a middle route where visibility remained good.

Nico looked around.

“You planned this.”

“I selected ground.”

“That is planning.”

“Yes.”

“Good. We are making progress.”

Flavio stopped near a marker stone.

A public sign identified the local route and warned players not to chase targets beyond the starter boundary.

Nico read it.

“Do people actually obey these?”

A shout came from farther ahead.

Someone had crossed the marked edge and was running back with two larger creatures behind him.

Flavio looked at Nico.

“Some learn.”

They moved away before the problem reached them.

The creatures stopped near the boundary and turned back.

Nico watched.

“So the warning was real.”

“Most useful warnings are.”

“That is suspiciously responsible.”

“I died responsible.”

Nico blinked.

Flavio felt the words leave his mouth.

He looked away.

Nico frowned.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That did not sound like nothing.”

Flavio checked the field.

The private interface had not appeared since entering Genesis.

Good.

He did not want to explain his dead first life beside a starter quest.

Nico let the subject go.

That kindness mattered.

Another Gnawer surfaced.

This one was alone.

Flavio and Nico approached carefully.

The creature noticed them.

Its head turned.

Its body lowered.

Flavio’s grip tightened.

Too much.

He relaxed his hand.

Don’t fight the tool.

Rogelio’s lesson fit a sword better than Flavio liked.

Nico drew his own weapon.

“Together?”

“Not yet.”

Nico looked at him.

“You want first contact.”

“Yes.”

“That sounds worse when you say it calmly.”

Flavio smiled faintly.

“I need to know what one hit feels like.”

“And if it bites you?”

“Then I learn what that feels like too.”

“That is not comforting.”

“You can step in if it gets bad.”

Nico’s expression changed.

“You mean that?”

“Yes.”

There had been a time when Flavio would rather collapse than ask someone else to carry part of the work.

Death had made that habit look stupid.

Nico nodded.

“Fine.”

The Gnawer moved sideways.

Flavio followed with his eyes, not his sword.

Its body paused.

Rear legs shifted.

He almost moved.

Then the creature only changed direction.

Flavio stayed.

Good.

No need to react to every twitch.

The creature circled.

Flavio adjusted his stance.

His body felt better inside Genesis than he expected, not stronger, just familiar enough that balance came naturally. A year of walking, jogging, lifting, catching, and stopping when tired had built something boring and useful.

Nico whispered, “It’s going to jump.”

“Maybe.”

“There. See?”

The Gnawer paused.

Flavio braced.

Nothing happened.

It moved left.

Then its rear legs compressed.

Flavio saw it half a second too late.

The creature launched.

He shifted.

Not enough.

Impact struck his thigh.

Pain flashed bright.

Flavio staggered.

His HP dropped.

Nico shouted.

“Flavio!”

“I’m fine.”

“You got hit!”

“I noticed.”

The Gnawer landed and turned.

Flavio backed away.

The pain was controlled, but real enough to force respect.

Good.

The thought surprised him.

Not because pain was good.

Because feedback was honest.

He had read the wrong cue.

That mattered more than being embarrassed.

Nico moved closer.

“Want me in?”

“Wait.”

The Gnawer circled again.

Flavio watched the body.

Not the pause.

Not the head.

Rear legs.

The creature shifted left.

Paused.

Nothing.

Moved right.

Rear legs compressed.

There!

Flavio stepped outside the line.

The Gnawer passed close enough that he felt fur brush his tunic.

He swung.

The beginner sword connected along the flank.

The creature rolled through the grass.

Nico shouted.

“Yes!”

Flavio almost answered.

Then the Gnawer scrambled up.

Still alive.

Flavio raised the sword again.

Nico lowered his voice.

“Right. No celebration.”

“Later.”

“If we live?”

“We have three lives per real day.”

“That was not permission to waste one.”

Flavio smiled.

“Good.”

The wounded Gnawer moved differently now.

Less testing.

More direct.

Flavio noticed because it rushed.

He angled away.

The creature missed.

He did not swing.

Nico groaned.

“You had it!”

“I had an opening.”

“That is what I said!”

“No. An opening is not the same as a safe hit.”

Nico looked offended.

“You are going to make combat sound like appliance repair.”

“Appliance repair has fewer teeth.”

The Gnawer circled again.

Flavio’s thigh still hurt.

His pulse was too fast.

His grip wanted to tighten every time the creature moved.

He let it loosen.

He was inexperienced.

That was all.

Not helpless.

Not skilled.

Just new.

The creature compressed its rear legs.

Flavio moved.

This time, before it left the ground.

The world narrowed to distance, footing, angle, and one ugly monster trying to bite him.

For the first time since entering Genesis, the game stopped feeling like something happening around him.

It became something he could learn.

E003-P03 — A Quest With No Mystery

The quest did not look important.

Flavio liked that immediately.

No glowing border.

No dramatic warning.

No mention of fate, hidden opportunity, or exclusive reward.

Just a public starter request sitting among other public starter requests while new players crowded the board and tried to decide which one would make them rich fastest.

East Verge Pest Control.

Objective: Defeat 8 Verge Gnawers.

Flavio read it twice.

Eight.

A useful number.

Not because eight sounded easy.

Because eight was specific.

Nico leaned over his shoulder.

“That one?”

“Maybe.”

“You said that about the last one.”

“I was right not to take the last one.”

“You never proved it was bad.”

“I didn’t need to.”

“That is very convenient logic.”

Flavio ignored him and read the quest again.

The words did not tell him how fast a Gnawer moved.

They did not tell him whether the starter sword was sufficient.

They did not tell him how damage felt.

They did not tell him whether eight creatures meant eight short fights or one very long mistake repeated eight times.

Good.

At least the quest was not pretending to tell him more than it did.

Several players around them accepted immediately.

One laughed.

“Pest control? Easy.”

Another player asked whether anyone had seen a Gnawer yet.

No one answered.

They ran anyway.

Flavio watched them leave.

Nico pointed after them.

“They are getting ahead.”

“Ahead of what?”

“The opening day.”

“That is not a location.”

“You know what I mean.”

Flavio did.

That was why he stayed where he was.

Opening-day pressure had the same smell as overtime pressure: everyone else is moving, therefore standing still must be failure.

He had already died once trying to outrun that feeling.

That did not mean he intended to spend Genesis sitting beside a quest board.

Flavio accepted.

[QUEST ACCEPTED]

East Verge Pest Control

Objective:
Defeat 8 Verge Gnawers.

Progress:
0 / 8

The window folded to the side of his vision.

No private message followed.

Flavio waited a second.

Nothing.

Nico noticed.

“There. Again.”

“What?”

“You keep waiting after system messages.”

“I’m reading.”

“You finished reading.”

Flavio started walking.

“You monitor me too closely.”

“You are suspicious.”

“That is different.”

“No, that is why.”

They followed the eastern route out of the busier district.

The change was gradual.

Stone streets widened.

Buildings thinned.

Vendors near the gate sold food, basic supplies, simple recovery items, and things Flavio could not afford even if he suddenly decided he needed them.

He slowed near one stall.

Nico sighed.

“You’re doing prices again.”

“Yes.”

“We still have almost nothing.”

“That makes the prices relevant.”

“It makes buying irrelevant.”

“For now.”

Nico looked at him.

“There it is.”

“What?”

“The merchant disease.”

“I have never been a merchant.”

“You are mentally selling things we do not own.”

Flavio glanced at a bundle of basic field rations.

The stall nearest the gate charged more than one they had passed several minutes earlier.

Convenience.

Traffic.

Players who reached the gate and refused to walk back.

He remembered the difference.

No notebook.

No lecture to himself.

Just memory.

That was enough.

They reached the eastern gate.

The stone road ended beyond it.

Packed earth, grass, low brush, drainage lines, uneven ground.

Flavio stopped before stepping through.

Nico nearly walked into him.

“Why did you stop?”

“Ground.”

Nico looked down.

“Yes. There is ground.”

“It changed.”

“You are observing dirt now?”

“I am observing where I’m putting my feet.”

Nico looked toward the field.

Players were already running.

One slipped in a shallow rut and caught himself badly.

Nico nodded.

“Fine.”

Flavio adjusted the satchel strap and checked that the sword would clear properly if he drew it.

Not because he expected a fight at the gate.

Because this was where a sword stopped being decoration.

He stepped through.

Wind moved across the verge.

The city noise remained behind them, close enough to hear but no longer surrounding them.

Players spread across the starter field.

Some stayed near the road.

Others went deeper immediately.

A shout came from the left.

Someone swung at a small creature and missed.

Another player laughed.

Then the creature bit him.

The laughing stopped.

Nico winced.

“That looked painful.”

“Probably was.”

“You are very comforting.”

Flavio watched the creature move.

Small.

Low body.

Fast enough that the player’s second swing came late.

He did not know whether it was a Gnawer.

He did not know whether all Gnawers moved the same way.

He kept walking.

The quest remained at zero.

Nico glanced at the interface.

“Eight is not that many.”

Flavio looked at him.

“You have not fought one.”

“True.”

“You do not know how long one takes.”

“True.”

“You do not know how much damage one can do.”

“True.”

Nico sighed.

“I regret talking.”

“That is progress.”

They moved farther from the gate.

Flavio chose a path with enough visibility to see movement before stepping into it.

He did not head for the densest cluster of players.

Too crowded.

He also did not go toward the emptiest stretch.

Not enough information.

Middle ground.

Nico followed.

“You planned that.”

“I picked space.”

“That is planning.”

“Fine.”

A public boundary marker stood beside the road.

Starter area.

Players advised not to chase creatures beyond the marked zone.

Nico read it.

“You think people obey?”

A figure ran past them toward the boundary after a fleeing creature.

Flavio watched.

“No.”

The player crossed.

A few moments later, he came running back.

Something larger followed him to the edge before stopping.

Nico stared.

“Ah.”

Flavio continued walking.

Warnings were often written in boring language because the consequences were expected to provide the drama.

Something moved ahead.

Both of them stopped.

Grass shifted.

Then became still.

Nico whispered.

“There.”

Flavio nodded.

A small creature emerged halfway.

Low body.

Coarse fur.

Front teeth large enough to make the name Verge Gnawer suddenly believable.

The official identification appeared after Flavio focused.

[TARGET IDENTIFIED]

Verge Gnawer

Level: 2

Rank: Common

Flavio read it.

Then dismissed it.

Nico looked at him.

“Level two.”

“We are level one.”

“That sounded worried.”

“It sounded accurate.”

The Gnawer moved sideways.

Quickly.

Flavio watched.

It paused.

Shifted again.

Paused.

Then disappeared behind grass.

Nico reached for his sword.

“Let’s go.”

“Wait.”

Nico stopped.

“Why?”

“Watch.”

“We are watching.”

“Longer.”

The creature appeared again.

It moved toward another player.

Flavio watched the body.

The player rushed.

The Gnawer changed direction before the swing landed.

Noted.

The player recovered.

The creature circled.

Flavio kept watching.

One example.

Not a rule.

The player eventually drove it away.

Nico turned.

“Okay. Now?”

“Not that one.”

“Why?”

“Someone else is already fighting it.”

“That is reasonable.”

Flavio looked at him.

“Sound surprised again.”

“I am getting used to it.”

They moved farther along the route until they found another Gnawer in a less crowded patch.

This one noticed them.

Its head turned.

Its body lowered.

Flavio’s heartbeat changed.

There it was.

The moment reading stopped being useful by itself.

He drew the starter sword.

The motion felt less graceful than it had in his room during practice.

Good.

Reality had arrived.

Nico drew his weapon too.

“Together?”

Flavio looked at the creature.

Then at Nico.

“I take first contact.”

Nico frowned.

“Why?”

“I need to know what it feels like.”

“That is not a reason I like.”

“If it gets bad, step in.”

Nico’s expression changed.

“You mean it?”

“Yes.”

The answer surprised both of them a little.

Flavio had spent one life treating help like a debt.

He had no intention of bringing that habit into combat if he could avoid it.

Nico nodded.

“Fine. I’m close.”

The Gnawer moved sideways.

Flavio kept the sword between them.

His grip tightened.

He loosened it.

Don’t fight the tool.

Rogelio’s voice did not arrive clearly.

The lesson did.

The creature paused.

Flavio almost moved.

It changed direction instead.

Good.

He waited.

Another pause.

Another shift.

Flavio watched the legs.

The shoulders.

The space between them.

He did not know the pattern yet.

That was the point.

The quest had promised eight creatures.

It had not promised the first one would cooperate.

The Gnawer moved closer.

Flavio adjusted his stance.

His body remembered the year of balance work, walking, jogging, stopping when tired, and learning where his feet were.

None of that made him a swordsman.

It did give him a body capable of staying underneath him while he was afraid.

That was enough for the first attempt.

The creature shifted.

Flavio raised the blade.

Nico went quiet behind him.

The first Gnawer was close now.

Close enough that Flavio no longer had the luxury of turning the moment into a lesson before living it.

He watched.

He waited.

Nico shifted his weight behind him.

Flavio heard the movement and raised one hand without looking back.

“Don’t crowd me.”

“I’m not.”

“You moved closer.”

“I moved supportively.”

Flavio almost laughed.

The Gnawer chose that moment to dart sideways.

His attention snapped back.

The humor vanished, but the looseness it left behind helped. His shoulders were no longer locked around his ears. His grip remained firm without crushing the handle.

This was what the year before Genesis had given him, he realized—not combat skill, not reflexes beyond reason, but enough awareness to notice when fear was turning his own body against him.

The creature paused.

Flavio waited.

Its nose twitched.

The shoulders lowered.

Still he waited.

Nico whispered something behind him, too quiet to understand.

Flavio did not ask.

The Gnawer moved again, drawing a shallow curve through the grass.

He adjusted only enough to keep it in front of him.

No chase.

No premature swing.

The sword felt strange in his hand, but not completely alien anymore.

He could work with strange.

He had done that for most of his life.

The creature turned sharply.

Distance collapsed.

Flavio felt the moment change from observation to action before he had words for it.

The quest had promised eight creatures.

It had not promised the first one would cooperate.

The Gnawer moved.

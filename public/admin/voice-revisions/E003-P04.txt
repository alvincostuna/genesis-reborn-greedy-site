E003-P04 — The First Gnawer

The Gnawer moved before Flavio decided whether he was ready.

That was probably useful.

If the creature had waited for him to finish thinking, he might have mistaken patience for control.

It darted left.

Flavio followed with the sword.

Too much.

The creature changed direction, and the blade cut through empty air where the Gnawer had been a heartbeat earlier.

Flavio pulled back.

Awkwardly.

His balance shifted farther than he wanted.

The Gnawer closed the distance.

He gave ground.

One step.

Then another.

Nico stayed behind and to the side, close enough to help without crowding him.

Good.

At least one of them was behaving sensibly.

The creature moved again.

Fast.

Low.

Ugly.

Flavio had seen enough rats in alleys to know that small did not mean harmless, but this thing had been designed by people who had apparently looked at a rodent and asked what would make its teeth more offensive.

He almost smiled.

Bad timing.

The Gnawer rushed.

Flavio raised the sword.

The attack came from lower than he expected.

He shifted.

Too late.

The creature struck his thigh.

Pain flashed through him.

Sharp.

Immediate.

Not real-world pain exactly, but convincing enough that his body reacted before his pride could.

Flavio stumbled half a step.

His HP bar dropped.

Nico moved.

“Flavio!”

“I’m fine.”

“You got hit!”

“I noticed.”

The Gnawer landed, turned, and began circling again.

Flavio backed away.

His thigh throbbed.

Controlled pain.

Still pain.

He could feel himself wanting to hurry now.

That old instinct.

Fix the mistake immediately.

Prove the first failure did not matter.

Hit harder.

Move faster.

End it before anyone could see he had been wrong.

Flavio hated how familiar that feeling was.

He loosened his grip.

The sword had not betrayed him.

He had swung at where the creature had been.

That was different.

The Gnawer shifted sideways.

Paused.

Flavio watched.

Nothing.

Then another shift.

Rear legs adjusted.

The creature compressed.

There.

It launched.

Flavio moved outside the line.

Not far.

Enough.

The Gnawer passed close to his hip.

He swung.

The blade connected along the creature’s side.

Flavio felt the contact all the way through his hand.

He nearly stopped to watch the result.

Rogelio would have hit him on the back of the head for that.

Not literally.

Probably.

The thought made Flavio recover his stance before the creature could turn.

The Gnawer rolled through the grass, scrambled upright, and faced him again.

Nico shouted.

“Yes!”

Flavio kept the sword raised.

“It’s still alive.”

Nico’s celebration ended.

“Right.”

The wound had changed the creature.

Less testing.

More direct movement.

Flavio saw that before he had time to explain it to himself.

The Gnawer rushed.

He stepped away.

It missed.

An opening appeared.

Flavio did not swing.

Nico made an offended sound.

“You had it!”

“I had space.”

“That is what I mean!”

“No.”

Flavio reset his feet.

“An opening is not automatically a good hit.”

Nico stared at him.

“You are going to turn combat into appliance repair.”

“Appliances have fewer teeth.”

The Gnawer circled.

Nico laughed despite himself.

Flavio’s pulse was still too fast.

His thigh hurt.

His sword hand was tightening again.

He relaxed it.

The first hit had told him something important without the courtesy of words.

The creature could hurt him.

Good.

Not good that it hurt.

Good that the game was honest about it.

A monster was not a moving bag of experience.

At least, not while it still had teeth.

The Gnawer paused.

Flavio watched the legs.

Another pause.

Nothing.

It moved right.

Rear legs gathered.

Flavio shifted before the launch.

This time the creature passed through empty space.

He started to swing.

Stopped.

The angle was poor.

Nico groaned.

“You stopped again!”

“Yes.”

“You already dodged it!”

“And?”

“And hit it!”

Flavio looked at him for half a second.

“That is how I got hit the first time.”

“No, you got hit because you missed.”

“Exactly.”

Nico opened his mouth.

Closed it.

“Fine.”

The Gnawer disappeared into taller grass.

Flavio listened.

Movement.

Left.

Then right.

He resisted the temptation to chase the sound.

When the creature surfaced several meters away, Flavio had enough room to breathe.

He checked his leg.

Still usable.

His HP had dropped, but not enough to force retreat.

That did not mean he had to continue.

He looked at Nico.

“You ready if I need you?”

Nico nodded immediately.

“Yes.”

No joke.

That mattered.

Flavio turned back.

The creature moved.

Its first feint pulled left.

He did not follow.

Shoulders dipped.

He waited.

Rear legs compressed.

There.

Flavio stepped outside.

The Gnawer launched past him.

He turned with it, kept the sword close, and waited until the safe angle opened.

Then he struck.

Clean.

Not dramatic.

Not heavy.

The blade met the creature and followed through without dragging Flavio off balance.

The Gnawer hit the ground.

Moved once.

Stopped.

Flavio remained ready.

He did not lower the sword immediately.

The field around him continued.

Players shouted elsewhere.

Wind moved through grass.

Someone laughed in the distance.

Nothing paused for his first kill.

The official quest did.

East Verge Pest Control

Progress:
1 / 8

Flavio stared at the number.

One.

Seven remaining.

That was all.

No trumpet.

No announcement that he had discovered a hidden combat talent.

Perfect.

He lowered the sword.

Nico exhaled loudly.

“You did it.”

“Yes.”

“You sound disappointed.”

“I got hit.”

“And then you won.”

“That does not erase getting hit.”

“It usually helps.”

Flavio looked at him.

Nico smiled.

“Come on. First monster.”

Flavio let himself breathe.

The adrenaline had nowhere to go now.

His hand trembled slightly.

He looked down.

That annoyed him.

Then he remembered the first time he had walked fifteen hundred steps and how his legs had complained as if he were asking them to cross a country.

Bodies reacted.

That was not failure.

He loosened the grip completely.

His fingers relaxed.

The sword suddenly felt heavier.

Flavio laughed once.

“What?” Nico asked.

“It weighs more now.”

Nico looked at the blade.

“It’s the same sword.”

“I know.”

“That makes no sense.”

“It does.”

Nico waited.

Flavio did not explain.

He looked toward where the Gnawer had fallen.

The creature’s body remained long enough for him to see the route of the last exchange.

He could almost reconstruct it.

First swing: too early.

Retreat: messy.

First hit received.

Second exchange: watched the wrong cue less.

First successful strike.

Then restraint.

Then final hit.

The memory was already trying to clean itself up.

Flavio noticed that.

He did not let it.

The miss mattered.

The pain mattered.

The hesitation mattered.

If he remembered only the final strike, the next monster would punish the same mistakes.

Nico walked closer.

“You are doing the face again.”

“What face?”

“The one where you turn an event into paperwork.”

Flavio looked at the field.

“I’m remembering.”

“That is allowed.”

“Thank you.”

“You’re welcome.”

A nearby player glanced toward them, then returned to his own fight.

Flavio appreciated the lack of attention.

His first victory did not need witnesses.

The quest count was enough.

He checked the official interface again.

1 / 8.

No experience settlement yet.

That stood out.

The kill had happened.

The quest had counted.

But the experience had not appeared.

Flavio looked for it once.

Nothing.

Nico noticed.

“What now?”

“No experience yet.”

Nico opened his own interface.

“Maybe delayed.”

“Maybe.”

“Or bugged.”

“Maybe.”

“Or you get it when the body disappears.”

“Maybe.”

Nico groaned.

“You are impossible.”

Flavio smiled.

He was not going to spend experience before he received it.

That habit had followed him through two lives.

Pending was not paid.

The creature finished fading.

Flavio waited.

No loot entered his inventory.

No item appeared.

Good.

He would not invent one.

He looked around for another Gnawer.

Then stopped himself.

His thigh still hurt.

His hand needed a rest.

The next target could wait.

Nico raised an eyebrow.

“You’re resting?”

“Yes.”

“Voluntarily?”

“Yes.”

“Inside Genesis?”

Flavio looked at him.

“You are enjoying this too much.”

“Very much.”

They moved toward a safer patch near the route marker.

Flavio sat.

The sword rested across his knees.

The quest remained active at one of eight.

Seven more.

That number could become a problem later.

For now, Flavio had something better.

One real fight.

One real mistake.

One real kill.

And, somewhere in the system, apparently, Nico handed him the starter water without being asked.

Flavio accepted it.

That small exchange bothered him in a good way. In his first life, he would have refused something he could fetch himself. He would have called it independence and wasted energy proving he still had two working hands.

Now he drank.

“Thanks.”

Nico looked at him suspiciously.

“You’re welcome.”

“What?”

“You said thanks without arguing first.”

“Do not make it strange.”

“You made it strange by becoming reasonable.”

Flavio ignored him and checked the quest again.

1 / 8.

The number did not flatter him.

It did not insult him either.

It simply waited.

Seven more could become a grind if he allowed the number to pull him forward faster than his body recovered. Seven more could also become practice, provided he survived long enough to learn from each one.

That difference would be his responsibility.

The private interface remained absent.

For once, Flavio was glad. He did not want another invisible system explaining what the fight meant. His thigh hurt. His hand had shaken. He had missed, adjusted, and eventually won. The scene had already said enough.

He looked at Nico.

“When we continue, you take one too.”

Nico blinked.

“I thought you wanted to test them.”

“I did.”

“And now?”

“Now we both need to learn.”

A grin spread across Nico’s face.

“That sounded almost like teamwork.”

“Do not ruin it.”

Nico laughed.

Flavio looked toward the grass again.

The next Gnawer could wait until Flavio’s hand stopped shaking.

The quest would still be there.

So would Nico.

And, somewhere in the system, apparently, one payment still on the way.

He looked toward the grass.

The next Gnawer could wait until Flavio’s hand stopped shaking.

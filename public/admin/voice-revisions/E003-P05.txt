E003-P05 — Ten Experience Points

The experience notice appeared while Flavio was still resting.

He had expected some kind of result after the kill, but expectation did not stop the number from catching his attention immediately.

[Experience settled: +10]

[Level: 1]

[Experience: 10]

[Experience required for next level: 400]

Flavio stared.

Ten.

He looked at the second number.

Four hundred.

Then back to ten.

“Ah.”

Nico turned.

“What?”

Flavio pointed at the display even though Nico could not see his exact screen.

“Ten.”

“Yes?”

“Four hundred.”

Nico opened his own status, apparently expecting context to arrive there.

“Oh.”

Flavio kept looking.

Ten experience from one Gnawer.

Four hundred needed for the next level.

He had no stat points to distribute.

No skill points.

No sudden burst of power.

The number simply existed.

Ten out of four hundred.

Nico looked at him.

“You’re smiling.”

“I’m not.”

“You are.”

Flavio’s eyes moved toward the grass.

Nico followed the look.

“No.”

Flavio turned back.

“What?”

“I know that face.”

“You said that earlier.”

“I was wrong earlier. I know it now.”

“What face?”

“The one where a number becomes personal.”

Flavio looked again at the field.

Seven Gnawers remained on the quest.

If each gave ten, that would be eighty from kills.

Maybe.

That assumption lasted less than a second before Flavio corrected himself.

One kill.

One settlement.

One value.

That was all he actually had.

He was not going to invent a guaranteed rate because the first number looked convenient.

Still.

Ten.

He flexed his sword hand.

The fight had not felt like ten.

It had felt like missed timing, a bruised thigh, a grip that kept tightening, and several moments when one small monster had controlled every part of his attention.

The clean notice looked almost rude by comparison.

+10.

As if the system had watched the whole mess and reduced it to a receipt.

Flavio understood receipts.

That helped.

He reopened the notice.

Still there.

Level one.

Ten experience.

Four hundred required.

Nico leaned closer.

“Checking if it changes?”

“Yes.”

“It won’t.”

“You don’t know that.”

Nico sighed.

“Fine.”

The number stayed.

Flavio closed the window.

Then opened it once more.

Same.

Good.

He did not need to check a fourth time.

Probably.

Nico watched him close it.

“Do you feel stronger?”

“No.”

“Faster?”

“No.”

“Smarter?”

Flavio looked at him.

“No.”

“That last one might be hard to measure.”

“Especially from your position.”

Nico laughed.

Flavio did too.

The tension left his shoulders a little.

He looked at the quest.

East Verge Pest Control

1 / 8

That number felt different from the experience total.

Same kill.

Different record.

One advanced the quest.

One advanced his character.

Neither meant the same thing.

Flavio did not need to explain that to himself.

He simply noticed both and remembered.

His thigh still hurt.

That was another record.

The system did not need to show it for the body to keep one.

Nico sat beside him.

“So what now?”

Flavio looked over the field.

“Rest a little more.”

Nico stared.

“Who are you?”

“Still me.”

“I am not convinced.”

Flavio ignored him.

Players moved across the Verge.

A group chased a Gnawer too close to another fight and nearly collided.

One player shouted.

Another apologized.

The monster escaped both.

Flavio watched.

Crowding was a cost too.

Opening hour did not mean empty space.

That would matter if he decided to stay here.

He looked at the experience total again mentally.

Ten.

Forty similar kills would make four hundred.

That arithmetic was easy.

Everything inside the assumption was not.

Would every Gnawer give ten?

Could he fight forty at this pace?

How much HP would he lose?

How long would recovery take?

Would there be better creatures?

Quest rewards?

Party experience?

Other sources?

Flavio stopped.

There it was again.

The old tendency to build an entire future from one receipt.

He shook his head.

Nico noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That is never true with you.”

“I was getting ahead of myself.”

Nico looked toward the field.

“With ten experience?”

“Yes.”

“That is impressive.”

“Not in a good way.”

Nico laughed.

Flavio relaxed his hand on the sword.

Rogelio had taught him to stop fighting a tool.

The same lesson seemed to apply to numbers.

A number told him what it was.

Ten.

Not forty kills.

Not a level.

Not a strategy.

Ten.

The rest had to be earned separately.

He looked toward the place where the first Gnawer had fallen.

The body was gone.

No loot.

No item.

No hidden reward.

Only the settled experience and quest progress.

That was enough.

Nico opened his inventory.

“Still nothing?”

“Nothing.”

“So monster drops aren’t guaranteed.”

“One example.”

“You really cannot let me generalize anything.”

“You can.”

Nico brightened.

“Really?”

“You can be wrong freely.”

Nico stared at him.

Then laughed again.

The field felt less frightening now.

That was dangerous.

Flavio noticed that too.

The first monster had hurt him.

The second might do worse.

Success had a way of erasing fear faster than skill actually improved.

He had seen that outside Genesis.

A repaired fan worked once, so someone stopped checking the wiring.

A worker survived one week of no sleep, so he believed the next would be fine.

A young man missed one family dinner, then another, then enough of them that absence became normal.

Flavio looked away from the memory before it grew larger than the moment.

He was here now.

Seventeen.

Alive.

Inside a game.

With ten experience.

Ridiculous.

He smiled.

Nico noticed.

“There. Again.”

“This one is allowed.”

“What are you thinking?”

Flavio considered lying.

Then answered.

“That ten is very small.”

Nico burst out laughing.

“That’s it?”

“Yes.”

“You nearly died to a rat for ten experience.”

“I did not nearly die.”

“It bit you.”

“It hit my thigh.”

“Fantasy rat violence.”

“Gnawer.”

“Ten-point fantasy rat.”

Flavio laughed harder than he expected.

The field around them continued as if nothing important had happened.

Maybe nothing important had.

One kill.

Ten experience.

Level one.

That was ordinary game progress.

But to Flavio, the number carried something else.

His effort had settled.

Not promised.

Not pending.

Settled.

He understood that feeling too well.

He had lived too many years on wages that existed first as promises.

Inside Genesis, the number had actually arrived.

He let himself appreciate that.

Then he stood.

Nico looked up.

“Ready?”

Flavio tested the injured leg.

The pain had eased enough to move.

His hand felt steady again.

“Yes.”

Nico stood.

“Next Gnawer?”

Flavio looked at him.

“Our quest says seven.”

Nico’s eyes narrowed.

“That was not my question.”

Flavio smiled.

“Next Gnawer.”

They moved away from the marker.

Not quickly.

Flavio watched the ground.

Watched the players.

Watched the grass.

The next fight would not be the first one.

That helped.

It also meant the next mistake would be his own.

Something moved ahead.

Nico reached for his sword.

Flavio did the same.

Before drawing, he checked the quest once.

1 / 8.

Then his status.

10 / 400.

Two honest numbers.

No story attached yet.

Flavio stepped toward the grass.

Ten experience points were not much.

That was fine.

He had always been good at starting with too little.
